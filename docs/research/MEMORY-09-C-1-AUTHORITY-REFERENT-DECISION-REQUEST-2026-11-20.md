**C1R-02 (Source-derived — the phrase).** §11's promotion paragraph (`:611`–`:614`):

> "Promotion per kind: episodic — on witnessing; preference — immediate for holder; belief — never promotable to

**C1R-03 (Calculated — why C-1 precedes the rest).** The referent decides **which questions exist**. If *authority*

not inside the fold.

## 3. The two readings

**C1R-08 (Calculated — the distinction, stated without preference).**

| | **R-E — epistemic warrant** | **R-D — deontic permission** |
|---|---|---|
| What "authority" names | the standing that makes the claim **credible** | the standing that makes the act **permitted** |
| What the condition ranges over | the claim and its support | the act of promoting and its actor |
| What would satisfy it | an authoritative source, or independent corroboration | a permission held by whoever causes the promotion |
| Grammatical fit | alternative to "corroboration" — both are warrants | a second kind of ground — but the sentence gives no actor, grant or check |
| What it is a property of | the **claim** | the **act** |

**C1R-09 (Source-derived — the sentence alone does not decide).** "Authority **or** corroboration" offers two
alternatives for the same promotion. Corroboration is plainly evidential, which makes a warrant reading natural
(NAB-06); but "authority" is a noun that can name either standing, and the sentence supplies no actor, no grant and
no check that would make the deontic reading grammatically impossible. **The sentence underdetermines the
referent.**

**C1R-10 (Observed — how r33 uses the noun).** Every occurrence of the noun is source-of-truth or epistemic standing:
"authoritative stream" (K-R12, `:111`), "Acquisition history is authoritative" (`:81`), "outside-authority failure"
(`:105`), "authoritative for knowledge" (`:320`), attribution kind `authoritative` (`:317`), D6's "unequal authority"
(`:74`), D7's "authoritative facts" (`:75`), I7's "retrievability does not confer authority" (`:293`), and I17's
"earns authority from the conditions under which it was established" (`:305`).

**C1R-11 (Observed — how r33 expresses permission).** Permission appears as "authorised *X*" or "who may":
D2's "authorised transfer" (`:70`), "authorised correction clears the group" (`:461`), and two **open questions** —
"decide who may author an `ambient_authored` record" (`:1024`) and "who may authorise deletion" (`:1031`).

**C1R-12 (Calculated — why that distribution is context, not a tiebreak).** Frequency of usage is not a definition.
The absence of the noun in permission clauses is exactly what one would expect if permission is expressed verbally
("authorised", "who may"), so the distribution does not show that §11's phrase is epistemic; it shows only that the
document's noun leans that way. **The request does not treat the count as an answer**, and the owner is free to read
the phrase against the document's wider verbal usage.

**C1R-13 (Observed — neither reading is a rule).** R-E and R-D are interpretations of one phrase. Neither is a
statement that promotions must be warranted, that permissions exist, or that any particular actor holds one.

**C1R-14 (Calculated — the readings are not exhaustive of the architecture).** Choosing R-E does **not** deny that a
permission question exists elsewhere; it says §11's phrase is not that question. Choosing R-D does **not** deny that
warrant matters; it says the phrase names the permission rather than the warrant. C-1 fixes **what the phrase
names**, not whether the other phenomenon exists.

## 4. What each reading would settle, and what it would not

**C1R-15 (Calculated — consequences, stated as implications of the reading, not as recommendations).**

| Consequence | Under **R-E** | Under **R-D** |
|---|---|---|
| **N-a separability at entry** | the condition is a semantic predicate; N-a can own it | the condition is a permission; N-a cannot own it, and the entry interface becomes authority-bearing |
| **Who owns it** | N-a, as a promotion predicate | no current owner: N-d excluded (NSD-04), DN-6a/b not applicable (NAB-12) |
| **Fold input** | a warrant recorded in attribution/evidence; evaluable under I-1, discharged at formation under I-2 | not evaluable from the log unless the permission is recorded — schema pressure — or it moves to the write gate |
| **New item exposed** | none | an unowned deontic question |
| **DN-6a/DN-6b** | untouched | untouched — the item is **not** theirs to absorb |
| **Validity predicate (DQ-4)** | not created | not created |
| **Fold shape** | not chosen | not chosen |

**C1R-16 (Calculated — the write-gate separation survives either way).** Permission governs whether an event may be
appended; the fold governs what an appended log means (K-R17's split; NAB-14/15). Under **both** readings the fold
can be specified independently of **permission**. The readings differ on whether the **entry condition** is itself a
permission, not on whether permission and projection are separable.

**C1R-17 (Calculated — neither reading selects an entry architecture).** R-E is compatible with derived entry (I-1)
and with given entry at formation (I-2). R-D is compatible with both as well, but changes what the **write gate** must
consult at formation. So C-1 does not choose between I-1 and I-2, and the request does not ask it to (NAB-18/19).

**C1R-18 (Calculated — R-D enlarges the boundary without filling it).** Under R-D, N-a is not separable from
transition authority **at entry**, but the dependency is an **unowned** item rather than DN-6a/DN-6b. That is the
opposite of the earlier fear: the scope determination is protected against leakage and exposed to an orphan
(NAB-35). C-1 does not decide what to do about the orphan.
