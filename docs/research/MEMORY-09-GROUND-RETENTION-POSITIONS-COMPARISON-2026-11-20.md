## 2. Are cause, ground and validity basis genuinely distinct?

**GRP-06 (Source-derived — cause).** D-3b, verbatim: *"Such events must retain **its actual cause** and
independently identifiable occurrence."* SD-02 reads that as the cause requirement; SD-18 characterises a cause
descriptor as naming "the *actor or mechanism* that caused the event", with its shape unsettled and whether it is
"a field, a relation or part of an event envelope" unanswered. CLV-30 lists **Cause** as one of six dimensions the
representation must not merge, with the question "why" and D-3b as its governing position.

**GRP-07 (Inferred — what a cause answers).** A cause answers **what produced this event**. It is a claim about
production. It does not answer whether the producing act was entitled to change anything, and it does not answer
whether the change was admissible for that kind.

**GRP-08 (Observed — ground).** "Ground" is **not canon vocabulary**: r33 contains no occurrence of it, and its one
use as a field label ("reason code", `:554`) is inside `[P]` §10.2.1 (TGP-05). No r33 provision requires a
transition to retain why it occurred (TGP-08).

**GRP-09 (Inferred — what a ground answers).** A ground answers **what basis was claimed for this change**. It is
first-person testimony about the act, authored by the process or party whose act it explains (TGP-20). That is why
it can raise auditability without raising reliability, and why the owner's boundary is drawn at exactly this point.

**GRP-10 (Source-derived — validity basis).** A validity basis answers **under what conditions this transition was
admissible**. Its components are partly in canon and partly absent:

| Component | Canon text | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Applicability** — does this kind of transition apply? | §11 `:611`–`:613`, "Promotion per kind: episodic — on witnessing; preference — immediate for holder; belief — never promotable to fact; rumour — stays provisional, repetition is not corroboration; world fact — authority or corroboration; absence — attributed" | stated for **promotion** per knowledge kind; **not** stated for the exit kinds, whose diagram glosses (`:606`–`:609`) define rather than condition |
| **Authority** — was the causing act entitled to change the state? | **ND-1 open** (ODL-32); "who may cause a lifecycle change is not established" (ODB-57) | **absent for lifecycle transitions**; specified for the promotion path (K8, §5) |
| **The principle** | I17 `:305` — authority comes from the conditions of establishment, never from when or where written | **present**; the predicate is not (CEV-24) |

**GRP-11 (Calculated — validity basis is not one thing either).** It decomposes into **applicability** and
**authority**, and they have different canonical states: applicability exists as per-kind prose for promotion only;
authority is entirely open for lifecycle transitions. A ground cannot supply either component, because both are
conditions the party recording the ground does not control (GRP-09; I17). This decomposition is why "validity basis"
cannot be treated as a synonym for "ground" and then dismissed along with it.

**GRP-12 (Calculated — the three, side by side).**

| | **Cause** | **Ground** | **Validity basis** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question answered | what produced this event? | what basis was claimed for this change? | under what conditions was this change admissible? |
| Who supplies it | the recording system, from the event's actual origin | the party whose act it explains | **nobody per transition** — it is a condition, evaluated against rules |
| Canon state | required for statement-less events (D-3b, SD-02); shape open (SD-18) | **not canon**; no requirement (TGP-08) | principle `[X]`; applicability per-kind prose for promotion; authority **open** (ND-1) |
| Granularity | per event | per transition (if retained) | per kind + per act's authority |
| Can it be self-certified? | yes — it is a report of origin | **yes** — by construction | **no** — that is I17's point |

**GRP-13 (Inferred — cause ≠ ground).** They can coincide (a review finding both causes a retraction and is its
stated basis) and diverge (a new fact causes a supersession while the basis is that the old record no longer
applies). D-3b delivers cause, and SD-18 leaves cause's shape open, so **cause may arrive without ground** (TGP-14).
The two must not be used interchangeably when the question is "why".

**GRP-14 (Inferred — ground ≠ validity basis, and canon says so somewhere else).** The ground is asserted by the
interested party; the validity basis is a condition that party does not control. That distinction is not merely
analytic — the **promotion path** states it in ratified requirements:

- **K13** — the basis kind is `record_cited | actor_declaration` and **is recorded**, or the promotion is refused;
- **K15** — **"Sufficiency is not assessed"**: a valid but weak basis is accepted, and "the actor's judgement
  [is] recorded verbatim";
- **K17** — declared content stored verbatim **with the declaring actor's identity and authority**;
- **K19/K20** — the basis is a set with no relevance or coverage test.

K15 is the operative sentence: canon **requires the basis to be recorded and forbids evaluating its sufficiency**.
That is the recording-versus-warrant separation, already realised as a requirement — for promotion.

**GRP-15 (Inferred — cause ≠ validity basis).** A cause can be recorded for an act that was not admissible: a
system-initiated retraction has a cause and, with ND-1 open, no established authority. Recording the cause does not
establish entitlement, and it cannot, because the cause is a fact about production while entitlement is a fact about
conditions.

**GRP-16 (Calculated — the distinctness verdict).**

1. **Cause and ground are distinct in principle** (GRP-13), and the corpus does not require them to be distinguished
   in any representation — because the ground does not exist there. Their distinctness is **available but
   unenforced** in the lifecycle stream.
2. **Cause and validity basis are distinct** (GRP-15), and this distinction *is* enforced for promotion (K8: the
   recording must not omit the authority relied on) and *is not* enforceable for lifecycle transitions, because the
   authority condition is what ND-1 withholds.
3. **Ground and validity basis are distinct** (GRP-14), and this is the one the owner's boundary turns on: recording
   a basis is not, and must not become, establishing a condition.
4. **Therefore the three are genuinely distinct concepts** — but only two of the three separations have canonical
   enforcement anywhere, and **neither separation is enforced in the lifecycle stream**. The architecture's nearest
   complete instance of all three coexisting is the promotion path, not L2.

**GRP-17 (Unestablished — the hinge).** Whether D-3b's cause descriptor, once shaped, **is a ground by another
name**. If it is, position 2 is a ground position and position 1 is unavailable (GRP-22). If it is not, position 2 is
a cause floor and the stream still has no ground. SD-18 leaves this open, and no record decides it.

**GRP-18 (Observed — K-section scope, stated to prevent over-reading).** K1–K27 govern **promotion of world-fact
claims into authoritative records**, not the lifecycle stream. Their relevance here is that they show the
architecture can state an authority condition (K8), a recorded typed basis (K13/K19), verbatim attribution (K17), and
a prohibition on assessing sufficiency (K15). They are **ratified** (map status: "RATIFIED AS REQUIREMENTS — O-6b")
and unchanged by this record. **Whether any part transfers to lifecycle transitions is tested in §5, and is not
assumed.**

## 3. What each position actually requires

**GRP-19 (Observed — the positions, as TGP-49 left them).**

| | Position | Content, as stated |
|---|---|---|
| **P-G0** | **No ground** | no transition retains any basis; "why" becomes unrecoverable; I17 cannot be violated by a ground because none exists |
| **P-G1** | **D-3b's floor** | cause on statement-less transitions only; retention is uneven by event class |
| **P-G2** | **Ground on every transition** | full auditability, full reader-misreading risk, largest surface on which the CLV-28 constraint must hold |

**GRP-20 (Calculated — they are not three points on one axis).** The three positions answer **two different
questions**, and one of them is already partly answered:

| Question | State | Which positions answer it |
|---|---|---|
| **RQ-1** — is a **cause** retained? | answered for statement-less events (D-3b); the boundary of "statement-less" is undrawn (TGP-11) | P-G1; P-G0 and P-G2 only insofar as they say anything about cause |
| **RQ-2** — is a **ground** retained? | **undecided** stream-wide (ODL-27) | P-G0, P-G1, P-G2 |
| **RQ-3** — is a **validity basis** recorded per transition? | **not a retention question**; no canon requirement; ND-1 open | **none of them** |

**GRP-21 (Calculated — why P-G0 and P-G1 are neither equivalent nor adjacent).** P-G0 says no ground is retained.
P-G1 says a cause is retained where a statement is absent. They coincide **only if cause is not a ground**
(GRP-17). If cause *is* a ground, then D-3b already places a ground on part of the stream and **P-G0 is not a
description of the corpus but a claim about vocabulary** — one that would have to displace an earlier owner decision
to be true. The owner's observation that the two "are not actually equivalent" is therefore exact, and its precise
ground is this unsettled identification, not a preference between them.

**GRP-22 (Inferred — why P-G2 is not "P-G1 everywhere").** P-G2 changes the **kind** of obligation, not just its
coverage: from a per-event *cause* rule (D-3b) to a per-transition *basis record*. It is the only position that would
make the stream self-describing about why, and the only one that would impose a stream-wide obligation no text
currently states (GRP-33). Its added coverage is **not** a widening of D-3b; it is a different requirement that
happens to cover the same events.

**GRP-23 (Source-derived — the D-3b minimum has three parts, and only one is a retention obligation).** The decision
says: *"Such events must retain **its actual cause** and independently identifiable occurrence. Absence of
conversational evidence must never be replaced with fabricated evidence or an inferred statement position."* Read
against SD-01…SD-04:

| Part | What it requires | Kind of obligation | Does a retention position satisfy or displace it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M-1 cause** | retain the event's actual cause (SD-02) | **retention** — a thing must be kept | the only part a ground position can be compared against |
| **M-2 occurrence** | independently identifiable occurrence (SD-03) | **identity** — the event must be itself | neither satisfied nor displaced by any position; already owed |
| **M-3 no fabrication** | no fabricated evidence, no inferred statement position (SD-04) | **prohibition** — a thing must not be done | binds every position equally, including P-G0 |

**GRP-24 (Calculated — the consequence for the comparison).** M-2 and M-3 are **not** options, floors or levels: they
are constraints that run alongside all three positions and are unaffected by the choice. So "the D-3b minimum" is
not a rung between P-G0 and P-G2. It is **one retention obligation (M-1) plus two constraints on how any record is
produced (M-2, M-3)**, and only M-1 belongs on the axis the owner is choosing.

**GRP-25 (Calculated — what each position requires of whom).**

| | Writer obligation | Fold obligation | Auditor/reader obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-G0** | none regarding basis; M-2/M-3 unchanged | none regarding grounds; derive only from transitions | must not read a missing basis as "no basis existed" (I15) |
| **P-G1** | record cause where the event is statement-less; M-2/M-3 unchanged | must not let the cause affect derived state (CLV-28) | must know the undrawn boundary before treating a missing basis as meaningful |
| **P-G2** | record a stated basis on every transition; M-2/M-3 unchanged | same prohibition, on every transition | must read every basis as *asserted*, never as established |

**GRP-26 (Calculated — the region with no requirement).** Between P-G1 and P-G2 lies the **statement-bearing**
transition: it has a source statement, so D-3b does not reach it, and no other provision requires a basis for it. Its
size is unestablished. P-G0 and P-G2 both resolve that region — by leaving it empty and by filling it — while P-G1
leaves it **as it is today: ungoverned**.

**GRP-27 (Observed — P-G2's obligation is genuinely new).** Checked against the lifecycle provisions: K-R12
enumerates the transition kinds (`:111`); §11 states the conditions under which promotion happens (`:611`–`:613`);
§11's parenthetical glosses define the exit kinds (`:606`–`:609`). **None requires that a transition record its
basis**, and none forbids it. P-G2 would therefore be an owner decision **adding** a requirement, not clarifying
one. That is a statement about provenance, not an objection.

**GRP-28 (Calculated — what no position requires).** None of the three requires, forbids or supplies: a validity
predicate; an authority condition; a closure declaration; an occurrence scheme; an identity scheme; an evidence
rule; or any change to the derived state. All three are silent on those, because none of them is a retention
question (RQ-3; GRP-20).

**GRP-29 (Calculated — three positions, three kinds of content).** P-G0 is a **silence**; P-G1 is a **decided
floor**; P-G2 is an **undecided addition**. That is why they cannot be placed on one scale, and why the comparison in
§4 is run per axis rather than per position.

## 4. What each preserves or sacrifices

**GRP-30 (Calculated — axes answerable from text).** Nine axes, each with a determinate answer per position. None is
a preference; each states what the position would require or expose.

| Axis | **P-G0** no ground | **P-G1** D-3b floor | **P-G2** ground on all |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Retained per transition** | nothing about basis | cause, where statement-less; nothing otherwise | a stated basis, on every transition |
| **B. Provenance of the obligation** | none — requires that cause not be a ground | **D-3b** (owner, 2026-11-19), applied as written | **none**; no K-R, invariant or §11 sentence requires it |
| **C. Adds a new obligation?** | no | **no** — already decided | **yes** — stream-wide, and new |
| **D. Fold / CLV-28 surface** | none | fold must not read the cause (TGP-38 unestablished in practice) | same, on *every* transition; largest surface |
| **E. I17 exposure** | none via grounds; absence-of-ground can be misread as "no reason existed" (TGP-46) | same, narrower; the unevenness becomes a fact readers must interpret | reader misreading exported to every transition (TGP-45) |
| **F. I15 exposure** | an absent ground must never mean "no reason existed" | a *new* absence is created for statement-bearing transitions | no per-transition absence; the truncation absence remains (LCM-31) |
| **G. Auditor can establish** | what changed, and when | what changed, when, and what produced it — for a subset | what changed, when, and what was claimed as its basis — for all |
| **H. Closes the validity gap?** | **no** | **no** | **no** |
| **I. Reversible in time?** | yes, with the caveat at GRP-36 | yes for statement-bearing events only | prospectively yes; not for records already written |

**GRP-31 (Calculated — axis H is identical across all three, and this is the comparison's main result).** No
retention position supplies a validity basis. P-G2 records *claims about* bases; it does not record *conditions*.
The gap is ND-1's, plus the missing predicate (CEV-24) and the missing admissible-transition set (LCM-24). Selecting
any retention level leaves axis H unchanged. This is why the three cannot be ordered by "which is more correct".

**GRP-32 (Calculated — axis B is not symmetric).** Only P-G1's content is already decided. P-G0's content is
"nothing", which is not an obligation and therefore needs no decision — except for the one it would displace
(GRP-21). P-G2's content is an obligation no text states (GRP-27). The three are a silence, a decided floor and an
undecided addition.

**GRP-33 (Observed — P-G2's stream-wide obligation is absent from canon).** Stated once more because the comparison
depends on it: K-R12 (`:111`) defines the kinds; §11 (`:611`–`:613`) states promotion conditions; §11's glosses
define exits. Nothing requires a recorded basis on a lifecycle transition.

### 4.1 Auditability

**GRP-34 (Calculated — what each makes recoverable).**

| | Recoverable now | Becomes decidable **later**, if a rule arrives | Never recoverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-G0** | what changed, in what order, and when | nothing about why | any basis for any transition |
| **P-G1** | as P-G0, plus what produced statement-less events | why a statement-less event occurred, if its cause is later interpretable against a rule | any basis for statement-bearing transitions |
| **P-G2** | as P-G0, plus the claimed basis of every transition | whether each claim matched the applicable rule — **if a rule exists** | whether any claim was *true* (C′ open) |

**GRP-35 (Calculated — retention does not make validity decidable, and does not raise the ceiling on it).** Under
TGP-44, a ground converts "unanswerable" into "decidable later". That is a claim about **the ground's own question**,
not about validity: an auditor with a full set of grounds and no applicable rule can compare claims only against
each other. P-G2 therefore maximises the **record**, not the **decidability**, and axis H stays fixed (GRP-31).

**GRP-36 (Inferred — the time asymmetry, recorded as a property and not as a tiebreaker).** A decision to retain
grounds cannot be applied retrospectively without manufacturing them: a basis written after the fact is not a record
of a contemporaneous claim, and its later addition would be the same species of error SD-04 forbids in the evidence
dimension. So P-G0's cost is paid at write time and is not recoverable by a later decision, whereas P-G2's cost is
paid on every transition from adoption. **This asymmetry is a fact about when each cost falls, not evidence that
either position is right**, and it is expressly **not** offered as a reason to prefer retention. Treating "we cannot
add it later" as an argument for adding it now would be decision-by-irreversibility, which ODL-17 rules out in the
same spirit.

### 4.2 Uncertainty

**GRP-37 (Calculated — two different failure shapes, and the positions do not share them).** CEV-29 distinguishes
degrading **traceability** from destroying the **state**. Applied here:

| | Effect of an unknown validity condition |
|---|---|
| **P-G0** | degrades traceability only; the state is still derived from transitions |
| **P-G1** | same, plus an uninterpretable absence wherever the boundary is undrawn |
| **P-G2** | same, plus a recorded claim whose truth is unknown and which readers may over-trust |

**GRP-38 (Inferred — absence means different things under each position, and only one of them is stable).** Under
P-G0 an absent ground is **uniform**: every transition lacks one, so the absence carries no per-transition
information and cannot mislead about a particular event. Under P-G1 the absence is **selective** and its meaning
depends on the undrawn boundary (TGP-11): a missing basis might indicate a statement-bearing event, or an event
whose cause was not recorded. Under P-G2 the remaining absence is **residual** — it signals truncation or an
incomplete write, not a design choice. I15 applies with full force in all three cases; **the risk shifts from
"absence read as no reason" (P-G0/P-G1) to "claim read as proof" (P-G2)**, and no position removes both (TGP-46).

**GRP-39 (Calculated — uncertainty is not reduced by any position).** The uncertainty that matters for the fold is
about the **history's completeness** (ODL-26, LCM-31) and the **act's authority** (ND-1). Grounds bear on neither.
P-G2 can present a fully annotated stream that is still truncated and still unauthorised, and a reader is *more*
likely to mistake annotation for completeness than to mistake silence for it.

### 4.3 I17 compliance

**GRP-40 (Calculated — what compliance requires, per position).**

| | What I17 requires here | Is the obligation vacuous? |
|---|---|---|
| **P-G0** | nothing about grounds, because none exist | **yes for the prohibitions** — no ground can be read as authority; **no for the positive requirement**, which remains unmet because no predicate exists |
| **P-G1** | the fold must not read the cause as standing; the reader must not either | **no** — the obligation attaches to the cause the moment it is carried |
| **P-G2** | same, on every transition, and for every reader | **no** — largest surface, same obligation |

**GRP-41 (Inferred — non-violation by omission is not compliance).** P-G0's I17 status is often stated as "I17
cannot be violated because no ground exists". That is accurate about the **prohibitions** and misleading about the
**requirement**: I17 also requires authority to be traced to the conditions of establishment (GRP-10), and no
position traces it, because the predicate and the authority condition are missing. P-G0 avoids one failure mode; it
does not satisfy the invariant.

**GRP-42 (Calculated — projection invariance is necessary, insufficient, and orthogonal to the choice).** The
three-row perturbation test (TGP-33) holds under all three positions: vacuous under P-G0, a real constraint on the
fold under P-G1 and P-G2. Passing it is **necessary and not sufficient** (B-4; TGP-34) because a fold can read a
ground in ways that never change the emitted state. It does not distinguish the positions.

**GRP-43 (Inferred — compliance is a property of the fold and the reader, not of the retention level).** Under P-G2
the system can be perfectly ground-blind and still emit, for every transition, an artifact a human reads as
justification (TGP-45). Under P-G1 that exposure exists only for the subset. Under P-G0 it does not exist, replaced
by the opposite exposure (GRP-38). **Each position converts one misreading risk into another; none removes the
class.** That is why I17 compliance cannot be used to rank them.

## 5. The promotion-path precedent: analogy or transfer?

**GRP-44 (Observed — what the precedent is, and its scope).** The ratified requirements for **world-fact promotion**
(K1–K27; map status "RATIFIED AS REQUIREMENTS — O-6b") contain the architecture's only complete instance of
recording separated from sufficiency:

| Requirement | What it states |
|---|---|
| **K8** | promotion by the world/scenario authoring authority succeeds **and records that authority**; any other actor is refused; failure if "the recording omits the authority relied on" |
| **K9** | the operation records actor, **authority**, time, domain, **applicability**, source; any missing field fails |
| **K13** | the basis kind ∈ {`record_cited`, `actor_declaration`} **and is recorded** |
| **K15** | **"Sufficiency is not assessed"**; a valid but weak basis is accepted; the actor's judgement recorded verbatim |
| **K17** | declared content stored verbatim **with the declaring actor's identity and authority** |
| **K19/K20** | the basis is a **set** of ≥1 typed entries; no relevance or coverage test |

**GRP-45 (Inferred — why it looks transferable).** It draws exactly the distinction this comparison is about: an
authority condition that is independently specified (K8), a basis that is recorded and attributed (K13/K17), and an
explicit refusal to treat the basis's sufficiency as established (K15). On its face that is "ground recorded, never
read as warrant" — the CLV-28 constraint — already in ratified text.

**GRP-46 (Calculated — component-wise transfer test).** Each component is tested for what it **presupposes** and
whether that presupposition exists for lifecycle transitions:

| Component | Requires | Presupposes | Presupposition present for lifecycle? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **K8** authority recorded | a named authoring authority, and the record must not omit the authority relied on | **that an authority is specified** | **No** — ND-1 open; who may cause a lifecycle change is not established | **Not transferable.** It is parasitic on precisely the decision that is missing. It cannot supply what ND-1 withholds |
