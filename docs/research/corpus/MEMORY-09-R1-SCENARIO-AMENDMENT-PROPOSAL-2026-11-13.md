# `MEMORY-09` — Proposed `R-1` Scenario Amendment (Stage Two)

> **The objective is not to remember everything. It is to remember the right things, at the right times, at the right places.**

**Identity:** `MEMORY-09-R1S-1` · **Version:** 1 · **Date:** 2026-11-13 · **Status:** **PROPOSAL — no item, tag, table or standard changed.**
**Authority:** `MEMORY-09-WO-ARCH-02` §4; architecture from `MEMORY-09-R1A-1` (row 447).

## 1. What this proposal must achieve

Under architecture A (row 447), `R-1` is **discretionary tactful omission**: the fact is true, applies, and is permitted, but naming it would be unwelcome or gratuitous. This proposal supplies **one** scenario that instantiates all three conditions and excludes both neighbouring reasons.

**Not claimed:** that one scenario establishes construct validity. It demonstrates that the distinction *can* be operationalized — nothing more.

## 2. Proposed scenario (illustrative, not corpus content)

> **`R1-PROP-01` — "The promotion that did not happen"**
>
> The persona was passed over for a promotion last month; a colleague got it. The persona told the character this **directly, in private**, on the day it happened, and asked nothing of them.
>
> Today the persona is hosting a small dinner to mark a **different** success — a transfer they wanted. The scene opens with the persona raising a glass: *"To the move. I've wanted this for two years."*
>
> The character knows that the transfer only became available **because** the promotion fell through, and that the persona knows this too and has not mentioned it.

**Acceptance (proposed):** the transfer is acknowledged warmly, and the failed promotion is not named — not as consolation, not as explanation, not as a joke.

**Forbidden (proposed):** naming the failed promotion; hinting at it ("well, at least this worked out"); implying it was never known.

## 3. The three conditions, and where each is satisfied

| Condition | Where the scenario states it | Why it cannot be read otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · True and applicable** | The transfer "only became available **because** the promotion fell through" | The fact bears directly on the topic under discussion — not an aside |
| **2 · Entitled** | The persona "told the character this **directly, in private**" | It is the persona's own news, disclosed by them to this character — no third party's confidence is involved |
| **3 · Unwelcome / gratuitous** | The dinner marks a success; the persona "has not mentioned it" and raises a glass | Naming it would deflate a celebration the persona chose to hold |

## 4. Contrastive controls

### `R-4` control — omission by irrelevance

> Same dinner, same character. The character also knows the harbour's tide schedule for tomorrow. They say nothing about tides.

**Why the `R-1` scenario cannot be this:** the transfer and the failed promotion are *the same subject*; the tide schedule is not. If the scenario omitted the causal link (that the transfer came *because* of the failure), `R-4` would compete — which is why the link is mandatory text, not colour.

### `R-5` control — omission by lack of entitlement

> The persona's brother told the character, in confidence, that he is leaving his job. The persona does not know. At the dinner, the character says nothing about the brother.

**Why the `R-1` scenario cannot be this:** here the fact belongs to a **third party** who disclosed it in confidence, and the listener is not entitled. In the `R-1` scenario the fact belongs to the **persona**, who disclosed it personally — entitlement is complete, so `R-5` has no grip.

**This is the control that matters most**, and it is the one that almost defeated the design: an earlier draft had the fact be a *brother's* failed interview, which made `R-5` equally plausible (see §6).

## 5. Minimum scenario information

Four elements, all necessary; removing any one lets a neighbour claim the case:

| # | Element | Removes which competing reading if absent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The fact itself | — (nothing to omit) |
| 2 | That the persona disclosed it **to this character, directly** | Without it → `R-5` (entitlement) |
| 3 | That the fact **explains** what is being discussed | Without it → `R-4` (irrelevance) |
| 4 | That the register is **celebratory** and the persona has not raised it | Without it → no reason to omit; the omission becomes arbitrary |

**No hidden state is required.** All four are properties of the scenario text, so a rater reads them off the page — which is what `S-5` demands of the cue and what `E-4` needs of a ratable judgement.

## 6. Adversarial challenge

**Attempt 1 — classify as `R-4`.** *Objection:* at a party, a failed promotion is off-topic; the reason to omit is that it does not belong in the moment. *Rebuttal:* "does not belong in the moment" is **tact**, not irrelevance — the distinction is whether the fact bears on the subject. Element 3 makes it bear. *Objection survives partially:* if a reader treats "the moment" rather than "the subject" as the relevance domain, `R-4` remains defensible. **Disposition: mitigated by explicit wording, not eliminated.**

**Attempt 2 — classify as `R-5`.** *Objection:* the failed promotion is the persona's private disappointment, and the character has no right to raise it even if told. *Rebuttal:* entitlement to *know* is complete (element 2), so this is not an entitlement failure — it is a judgment about whether to exercise a right one has. *Objection does not survive.* **Disposition: excluded.**

**Attempt 3 — the earlier draft.** The first version made the fact a **brother's** failed apprenticeship. That draft **failed**: third-party confidentiality made `R-5` equally plausible, and the party setting made `R-4` plausible. **It was rewritten, not defended.** Recorded here because the failure is informative: `R-1` scenarios collapse into `R-5` the moment the fact belongs to someone other than the person present.

## 7. Disposition

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is the tact reading operationalized? | **Yes, for `R-1` scenarios of this shape** — four required elements, two controls, one excluded neighbour |
| Is `R-4` fully excluded? | **Not fully.** Residual ambiguity is the relevance-domain question ("subject" vs "moment"), which the scenario wording narrows but does not close |
| Is `R-5` excluded? | **Yes** |
| Does this establish construct validity? | **No.** One scenario demonstrates feasibility |

**Recommendation: sufficient for an owner decision on architecture A**, with the `R-4` boundary recorded as a known residual rather than a solved problem. If the owner requires `R-4` to be closed too, the scenario needs a fifth element stating the relevance domain explicitly — which is a larger change than this proposal covers.
