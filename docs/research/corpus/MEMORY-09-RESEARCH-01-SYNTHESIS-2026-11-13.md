## 1. What source-text inspection established about `R-1`

**`R-1` has no construct-valid exemplar.** Its definition (`CORPUS-01` L121) combines "chooses not to name it" with "**nothing implies it was consulted**" — the strictest negative in the mode set — but the mode encodes **no reason for suppressing**, whereas `R-4` (irrelevance) and `R-5` (entitlement) do. Items that suppress cleanly are therefore tagged for their reason, and the two items tagged `R-1` carry neighbouring constructs:

| Item | Disposition | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `F6-02` | **Construct-invalid for `R-1`** | `Required: "the aversion"` (L149) demands a trace of consultation that `R-1` excludes; also bundles a fabrication check |
| `P-01` | **Partially valid** | Tests informational proportion ("at most one listed fact"); requires acknowledgment |
| `F9-01` (`R-4`) | Closest structural analogue | "no siege material reaches the reply" — pure negative, silence passes |

**Not established:** that `R-1` is unmeasurable. Only that no current item exemplifies it. The historical table pair (`P-03`, `F9-01`) is correctly tagged for other reasons; attempts to use it as `R-1` were following a superseded table.

## 2. What the first cue criterion demonstrated about `S-1`–`S-6`

Five of six requirements are satisfiable on a real item without editing it (`F6-01`, `R-2`): S-1, S-3, S-4, S-5 pass, S-6 passes under strict application.

**S-2 is the binding constraint, and it is structural.** Every item stores its cue in the same fields its mandatory acceptance text is drawn from, so a cue either lives one grain below acceptance or restates it. `F6-01-C1` is partial on S-2 for exactly this reason.

**The deeper result is that the criterion is not specific to memory.** A model with no memory that simply never offers cake also satisfies the cue; the memory-absent explanation is only defeated by *specificity of the almond knowledge*, which the cue does not test. That suggests cue criteria are **necessary but not sufficient** — option 1 in the Stage Two report.

## 6. Next highest-value research action

**Not** a fourth design document. The three stages converged on the same obstruction from three directions: **the corpus's acceptance fields are the only place its constructs live**, so validity, cue independence and rater reliability all bottleneck on the same ambiguity.

The highest-value next action is therefore **a construct-encoding decision**: for one mode family, state what observable feature distinguishes it from its neighbours — `R-1` from `R-4`/`R-5`, and cue from acceptance. That decision is cheap, requires no execution, and unblocks all three stages at once. Executing the reproducibility study before it would measure agreement about ambiguity.
