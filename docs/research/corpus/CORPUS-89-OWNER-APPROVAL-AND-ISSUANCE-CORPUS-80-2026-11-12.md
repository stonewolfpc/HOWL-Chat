## 5. Effect on `CORPUS-81` — recorded, not decided

Issuance activates the Q1 charter. It does **not** convert `CORPUS-81` into an authorized deliverable:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Was `CORPUS-81` commissioned? | **No.** It was produced against a draft (`CORPUS-84` §4.2), and `CORPUS-87` §3 confirmed issuance would not have changed that |
| Does it now become the Q1 deliverable? | **Not by this instrument.** The owner authorized issuance of the work order and adopted no finding. Nothing here assigns Q1's deliverable status |
| Is it independent? | **No**, by its own declaration, preserved verbatim (`CORPUS-84` §4.3) |
| Does it count toward convergence? | **No.** Convergence remains **0 of 2** |
| May Q2/Q3 cite it? | **Yes, as an input**, with both membership readings stated (`CORPUS-85` §3, `CORPUS-86` §3). Never as a premise |
| Is Q1 therefore complete? | **No.** Q1 has a charter and no commissioned adjudication. Whether `CORPUS-81` is accepted as satisfying any part of Q1 is an **owner** decision not yet taken |

**Consequence stated plainly:** the work order is now in force while Q1 has no authorized deliverable. That is a real sequencing gap, not a formality — `CORPUS-80` §9 makes Q1's outcome condition how Q2 and Q3 are read, so Q2 and Q3 will either wait or proceed under the dual-reading rule. Choosing between those is the assigner's call at assignment time, and the dual-reading rule is written into both briefs precisely so the choice does not silently become an adoption of Q1.
