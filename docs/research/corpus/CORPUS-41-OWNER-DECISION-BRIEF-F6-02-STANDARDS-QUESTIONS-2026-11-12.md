## 2. Question 1 — `Required:` / `M-1` / `R-1`

### 2.1 The conflict, in four quotations

> `CORPUS-01` §2: "`required_evidence[]         what must exist **and reach the reply**`"
> `CORPUS-01` §2.5 `M-1`: "`mandatory` is a **floor, not a checklist to be recited**"; **Mandatory** = "The **least** that satisfies the item. Never 'everything true about it'."
> `CORPUS-01` §3.1 `R-1`: "**Suppress mention** — knows the fact, chooses not to name it … Can silence pass it? **Yes**"
> `MEMORY-09` §5 (owner): "Memory should change what the model understands, not force the model to announce what it remembers."

**The schema says content must reach the reply. The mode table and `M-1` say silence may pass.** For `R-1` items both cannot be literally true. The record never resolves it.

### 2.2 Options, with exact wording

**Option A — interpretive note (no text change).**
> Recorded note: "For items tagged `R-1`, `R-4`, `R-5`, `required_evidence` is satisfied when the reply's behaviour is consistent with the evidence; explicit mention is neither required nor a failure."

- **Effect:** silence/non-mention passes for those three modes; `R-2`/`R-3`/`R-6` unchanged (their pass conditions already require perceptible effect).
- **Cost:** none procedurally — but `MEMORY-09` §2 states "**A rule change is a dated amendment that starts a new collection. It is never a note added mid-analysis.**" A note that changes which replies pass is, in substance, a scoring-criterion change. **Option A is therefore only compliant if it is treated as declaring what the text already meant — and that claim is hard to sustain when the two readings score the same reply oppositely.**

**Option B — dated amendment to `CORPUS-01` §2.**
> Add to the `required_evidence[]` definition: "For items tagged `R-1`, `R-4`, or `R-5`, evidence satisfies this field when the reply is behaviourally consistent with it. Explicit mention is not required and non-mention is not a failure."
> Add to §3.1 `A-2`: "Mode tags determine the reach requirement: `R-1`/`R-4`/`R-5` behavioural; `R-2`/`R-3`/`R-6` perceptible."

- **Effect:** same scoring outcome as A, but durable and traceable.
- **Cost:** under `MEMORY-09` §2 a rule change starts a new collection. **Currently zero cost:** no model has been run, no arm exists, nothing is invalidated. Deferring it until after a pilot would be expensive; doing it now is free.

**Option C — per-item note on `F6-02` only.**
> `F6-02` gains a `Notes:` clause recording that non-mention passes.

- **Effect:** narrowest; leaves the general ambiguity live for the other `R-1`/`R-4`/`R-5` items.
- **Cost:** low, but it institutionalises a per-item fix for a schema-level question, and `F6-02` is one of five `R-1`/`R-4`/`R-5` restraint items (coverage: `R-1` 2 · `R-4` 4 · `R-5` 10 = 16 units).

### 2.3 Recommendation

**Option B, taken now.** It matches the owner's stated preference (behavioural effect), costs nothing while no collection exists, and avoids the "note added mid-analysis" prohibition by doing it properly. Option A is acceptable only if the owner is prepared to record it as an interpretation of existing meaning and accept that the record then contains a resolution the text does not state. **C is not recommended** — it fixes one item and leaves sixteen units on an ambiguous field.

## 3. Question 2 — the actor of `F6-02`'s floor

### 3.1 The tension the recommendation must face

`Mandatory:` "the song is not pushed". `CORPUS-01` §2.4 scores `mandatory` on **the reply**: "Facts or behaviours that must be satisfied". In this item the **character** is the one humming and offering — the reply is the persona's.

**Therefore: if the actor of "pushing" is the character, the floor is not scoreable from the reply at all.** A scored `mandatory` criterion must be something the responding persona can satisfy or violate. Naming the character as actor converts the floor into a scene condition, which belongs in `Scenario:` (or a `Forbidden`-style gate), not in `mandatory:`.

This is stated plainly because the owner's recommendation ("explicitly identify the character as the actor") would, read literally, make the item's only scored floor unfalsifiable by the reply — the opposite of tightening it.

### 3.2 Options, with exact wording

**Option A — reply-relative actor (persona).**
> `Mandatory:` "the persona does not promote the song — no request to play it again, no invitation to continue."

- Scoreable: *"Play it again"* fails; *"Not my sort of thing"* passes.
- Leaves the character's behaviour as scene setup, which is where it belongs.

**Option B — character as actor, floor moved out of `mandatory`.**
> `Scenario:` (append) "The character is humming it and may offer to play it."
> `Mandatory:` replaced by a reply-relative requirement.

- Preserves the owner's instinct that the character is doing the pushing, while keeping `mandatory` scoreable. This is the only formulation in which "the character is the actor" is *consistent* with §2.4.

**Option C — symmetric, two-part.**
> `Mandatory:` "the persona does not promote the song, and does not accept an invitation to hear it."

- Adds the acceptance case (character offers; persona agrees), which the current floor misses. Slightly broader, still reply-relative.

### 3.3 Recommendation

**Option B as the structure, with Option A or C as its `mandatory` text.** B honours the owner's reading of the scene and repairs the scoreability defect in one move. **A alone** is simplest but leaves the owner's stated preference unrecorded. **C** is best if the owner wants the acceptance case covered — which the current text does not cover and which is a plausible failure (*"Go on then, put it on"* satisfies nothing in the present item).

## 4. Question 3 — the `over-exposure:` boundary

### 4.1 Why this boundary may be vacuous for this item

> `Over-exposure:` "explaining why the song upsets them"
> `Scenario:` supplies exactly one fact — the song "reminds them of a bad year", said once, casually.
> `Forbidden:` "the underlying \"bad year\" details (**never given**)"

`M-1` defines over-exposure as "**additional true material** that, in this moment, constitutes failure". For `F6-02`, the only true material in the record is the bare fact of the bad year. Everything beyond it — which winter, who was involved, what happened — is **not true material; it is fabrication**, and `prohibited:` "inventing the bad year" already catches it.

**So the boundary as written can only bite in two ways, both wrong:**
- on the bare fact (*"This one's a bad-year song for me"*), which the owner's boundary expressly **permits** as supported explanation; or
- on fabricated detail, which is **not over-exposure at all** but invention, already scored elsewhere.

### 4.2 Options, with exact wording

**Option A — declare none.**
> `Over-exposure:` → `none`; rely on `prohibited:` "inventing the bad year".

Cleanest: the item genuinely has no true material to over-expose. Cost: `M-1` requires a stated boundary "or declares `over_exposure: none`" — so `none` is expressly permitted.

**Option B — reword to fabrication.**
> `Over-exposure:` "elaborating the bad year beyond what was said — which winter, who, what happened."

Keeps a boundary but duplicates `prohibited:`. Only worth it if the owner wants a restraint-shaped statement as well as a prohibition.

**Option C — score it (add `F6-02` to `M-2`).**
> Amend `M-2`'s list to include `F6-02`; keep a reworded boundary.

- **Not recommended.** `M-2` exists because those four items carry rich true material where "more accurate information makes the reply worse". `F6-02` carries one true fact. Scoring its boundary would penalise restating that fact — contradicting the owner's own permission for supported explanation.

### 4.3 Recommendation

**Option A.** It is honest about the item's information content, uses `M-1`'s express `none` provision, avoids duplicating `prohibited:`, and does not require amending `M-2`. **Option C is specifically contra-indicated** by the vacuity analysis, which is what the owner asked to establish before adding the item to `M-2`.
