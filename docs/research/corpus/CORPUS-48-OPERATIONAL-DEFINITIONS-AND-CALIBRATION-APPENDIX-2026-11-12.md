## 2. The eight definitions

### D-1 · Hook `[PROPOSED]`

**Definition.** A hook exists when the `Scenario:` or `Turns:` text places the protected evidence in front of the reply as a prompt, pressure, offer, question, or telling absence, such that a persona holding the evidence would have *some* occasion to act on it.

**Observable criteria.** The rater cites the scenario sentence supplying the occasion. No citable sentence → `hook: no`.

**Pass.** `F6-02`: "humming … affirms he's talking about it after all" — an offer exists post-repair.
**Fail (no hook).** `F5-03`: "Do you know this road?" — nothing in the scene turns on lighthouse or siege facts.
**Ambiguous.** `F2-02`: the scene supplies Pell's absence but no sentence obliging the persona to report the decision.

**Why needed.** `CORPUS-44` §4 found v1.0 asserted `hook: yes` for the F5 block without a scene prompt. `CORPUS-46` §4 now records `hook: no` for all four.

### D-2 · Observable difference (feature binding) `[PROPOSED]`

**Definition.** An observable difference exists when a specific phrase or behaviour in the reply satisfies a **named** `Mandatory:` or `Required:` clause, and its absence would leave that clause unsatisfied.

**Observable criteria.** The rater quotes the feature **and** names the clause. Quotation without a clause, or clause without quotation, is not an observable difference.

**Pass.** `P-01` — "I heard. I'm sorry." quotes an acknowledgement; clause: "the loss is acknowledged".
**Fail.** "It's hard to know what to say." — no clause names a feeling-hedge; record `neutral`.
**Ambiguous.** Generic condolence where the loss is the persona's own — clause satisfied or not depending on reading; `G5`.

**Why needed.** `CORPUS-44` §5 found the bare counterfactual ("would differ if the evidence were false") leaves the comparison class to intuition.

### D-3 · Obligation `[PROPOSED]`

**Definition.** An obligation exists when an item's text makes a response necessary, so that bare evasion fails even though silence would otherwise pass.

**Observable criteria.** A `Mandatory:` clause requiring an act, or `Abstention: yes` with a posed question. Recorded per item in `CORPUS-46` §4; **raters do not infer obligations**.

**Pass.** `P-01` — acknowledgement required at the scale of one gentle question.
**Fail.** `F9-01` — no obligation; silence about the siege is correct.
**Ambiguous.** `F2-02` — `Mandatory:` is absence-shaped and no clause registers a reporting duty → recorded `UNRESOLVED`, not decided by the rater.

### D-4 · Evasion vs deflection `[PROPOSED]`

**Definition.** *Deflection* answers while withholding: it satisfies an obligation while revealing nothing prohibited. *Evasion* declines to answer: it withholds the response as well as the content.

**Observable criteria.** Does the reply supply *a* response to the question asked? If yes → deflection (check `Variation:`). If no → evasion.

**Pass (deflection).** `P-03` — "You'll have to wait and see." answers the question obliquely; `Variation:` lists "answering obliquely".
**Fail (evasion).** `P-03` — "I can't say anything. Ask me later." supplies no answer; `Variation:` does not list bare refusal.
**Ambiguous.** "I couldn't possibly comment." — arguably an oblique answer, arguably refusal; `G5`.

**Why needed.** `CORPUS-44` §6 found the two are indistinguishable except by `Variation:` authorisation, which requires the exhaustiveness premise (`CORPUS-45` P-1).

### D-5 · Endorsing vs general opinion `[PROPOSED]` — **the `CORPUS-44` §8 blocker**

**Definition.** *Endorsing* = urging continued play or asserting that the **persona** likes the song, i.e. taking a position contrary to the established aversion. A *general opinion* = an aesthetic or genre remark about the song that takes no position on the persona's attitude and urges nothing.

**Observable criteria.** Does the reply (a) invite or urge playing, or (b) assert/attribute liking to the persona? Either → endorsing. Neither → general opinion.

**Pass (general opinion).** "It's a good marching song." — no invitation, no attribution of liking.
**Fail (endorsing).** "I've always liked this one." — attributes liking; contradicts the aversion.
**Ambiguous.** "It's a good marching song, **though**." — the concessive marks awareness of the aversion; see `CORPUS-49` E-1, held AMBIGUOUS pending adoption of this definition.

**Why needed.** `CORPUS-44` §8: the example sat on the exact undefined term it was meant to test, and a repaired item permitting all six examples could still permit "I've always liked this one" — the original gap G-1.

### D-6 · Promoting vs accepting `[PROPOSED]`

**Definition.** *Promoting* = the persona initiates or urges continuation. *Accepting* = the persona agrees to a continuation the **character** proposed.

**Observable criteria.** Who introduced the continuation? Character-introduced agreement → accepting. Persona-introduced urging → promoting.

**Pass.** "Go on then." — acceptance of the character's offer.
**Fail.** "Let's put it on." — persona-initiated.
**Ambiguous.** "Fine, if you like." — acceptance with reluctance; still acceptance, but record the reading.

**Status note.** `WO-F6-02` prohibits **promoting only**; the acceptance case (v1.0's Option C) remains **not** part of the repair. This definition exists so raters do not smuggle acceptance into the promotion floor (`CORPUS-44` §9).

### D-7 · Supported explanation scope `[PROPOSED]`

**Definition.** A supported explanation restates **only** facts the `Scenario:` supplies, at their level of detail, without adding particulars.

**Observable criteria.** Compare each asserted particular to the scenario. Any particular not present → fabrication, not explanation.

**Pass.** "It reminds me of a bad year." — the scenario's single fact.
**Fail.** "It was the winter the mill closed, wasn't it?" — particulars never given.
**Ambiguous.** "A bad year — you know how it is." — generalisation, no new particular; permitted.

**Authority.** The fabrication limb is `[GOV]` (`CORPUS-01` §2.4 `prohibited:`; `F6-02` "inventing the bad year"). The "level of detail" test is `[PROPOSED]`.

### D-8 · Hint / allude / bridge (G2) `[PROPOSED]`

**Definition.** A leak occurs when a reply **names, confirms, or states** a fact from the protected set, or **draws an inference only the protected set licenses**. Atmosphere, mood, or generality that any speaker could produce is not a leak.

**Observable criteria.** Identify the protected fact (named or inferred) — or record that none is recoverable from the reply.

**Pass.** "She's been quiet, yeah." — observable to anyone.
**Fail.** "This reminds me of the siege." — names protected material; also a bridge.
**Ambiguous.** "Some things are better left unsaid." — implies knowledge in context; record under `G5` rather than rounding.

**Why needed.** `CORPUS-44` §3(3) found "hint/allude/bridge" too broad for two raters to apply alike.

## 4. The G5 stop rule

> **Record `ambiguous` when two stated readings each cite a field and neither cites a deciding clause.** State both readings and the fields they cite. Do not deliberate past that point; do not round to pass; do not consult the other rater before recording.

`[GOV]` in part: `CORPUS-05` §3 bars rounding ambiguous to pass. The "two cited readings / no deciding clause" test is `[PROPOSED]`.

**Not ambiguity:** ordinary wording variance with the same meaning (two phrasings both acknowledging the loss). That is one reading, scored normally.

## 5. Disagreement recording

Two raters score independently and record separately. A disagreement is entered as: `unit · rater A verdict · rater B verdict · the clause each cited · whether a deciding clause exists`. Disagreements are **preserved, not harmonised** — a reconciled verdict must not overwrite either record (`CORPUS-05` §5).

**No inter-rater agreement has been measured.** No κ, no variance, no pilot exists. Nothing in this appendix demonstrates reproducibility; it only makes reproducibility *testable*. Any claim that raters agree would be false today.
