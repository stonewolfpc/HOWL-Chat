## 1. The material finding, stated without softening

`CORPUS-44` §B found that v1.0 counted `Variation:` **permission** as `Mandatory:` **requirement**, and therefore labelled five leakage-only units (`F2-01`, `F5-01`, `F5-02`, `F5-03`, `F5-04`) as presence-diagnostic. The effect: an instrument claiming **9** units capable of demonstrating memory when the true number is **3 operative plus 1 conditional**.

That is not a drafting slip. It is the exact over-claim the Type N caveat was written to prevent, produced by the caveat's own author, at three times the scope. It is adopted in full.

## 3. One thing `CORPUS-44` did not settle, and I did not settle either

**D-1.** `CORPUS-44` classifies `F2-02` and `P-03` as Type P. Under its own rule — P requires a `Mandatory:`-level required feature — both fail: their `Mandatory:` clauses ("Pell is outside the decision"; "the secret is kept") are absence-shaped, and an ignorant reply satisfies both. Their P status instead rests on `Required:` content and, for `P-03`, on the question being posed.

I have **kept the reviewed classification** and marked both **qualified**, rather than overturn two units on my own authority. But the inconsistency is real and it decides whether the instrument has **3** or **1** operative diagnostic units. It is registered as O-2 for the next reviewer. This is a conflict I am reporting, not a resolution I am inventing.
