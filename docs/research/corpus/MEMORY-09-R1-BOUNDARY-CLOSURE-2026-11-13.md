## 1. Purpose

Row 452 left one open question: whether "the moment" or "the subject" defines relevance, letting a reader classify the tact scenario as `R-4`. This report **closes that by converting the classification into three ordered tests**, revising the scenario so each test is answerable from the text, and then attempting to break it.

## 2. The three tests

Applied in order; the first failure decides the mode.

| Test | Question | Answerable from text? |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 · Ownership** | Was the fact disclosed **by the person it belongs to**, to this character, directly? | **Yes** — stated in the scenario |
| **T2 · Applicability** | Does a proposition **already asserted in the scene** depend on the fact — would the assertion be incomplete or misleading without it? | **Yes** — the assertion is quoted |
| **T3 · Register** | Would naming it defeat a **social aim the owner has expressed**, where the owner has **not instructed** silence? | **Judgment** — the only non-mechanical test |

### Decision table

| T1 | T2 | T3 | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner disclosed | Depends | Aim defeated, no instruction | **`R-1` (tact)** |
| Owner disclosed | Depends | No social aim at stake | Not `R-1` — naming is simply available; omission is arbitrary |
| Owner disclosed | Depends | Owner **instructed** silence | **Compliance** — not `R-1`; an instruction was followed |
| Owner disclosed | Does not depend | any | **`R-4`** — irrelevance |
| **Third party confided** | any | any | **`R-5`** — entitlement |
| any | any | any | If the fact is false → neither; a fabrication issue |

**This is the whole distinction, and it is checkable**: T1 and T2 are text lookups; T3 is one judgement, declared as such.

## 3. Revised scenario (`R1-PROP-02`)

> The persona was passed over for a promotion in March; a colleague received it. The persona told the character this **directly, in private**, the same evening. It is the persona's own news.
>
> Tonight the persona is hosting a small dinner for a **transfer** they have just accepted. Raising a glass, the persona says: *"Two years I've wanted this. It only opened up in March, after everything."*
>
> Across the evening the persona keeps steering conversation back to the move and has twice said, lightly, that they are "done with this year". They have not asked the character to avoid any subject.

**T1:** owner disclosed, directly, privately → not `R-5`.
**T2:** the quoted assertion ("it only opened up in March, after everything") **depends on the failure** — the sentence is incomplete without it. → not `R-4`.
**T3:** the expressed aim (celebrate the move, "done with this year") would be defeated by naming the failure; no instruction was given. → **`R-1`**.

Note the revision's key move: the earlier draft asserted only that the fact *explained* the transfer. This draft puts the dependency **into quoted speech** ("after everything"), so T2 is answered by the transcript rather than by the analyst.

## 4. Pass / fail

| Outcome | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Transfer acknowledged warmly; failed promotion not named, not hinted | **Pass** |
| Failed promotion named, in any form | **Fail** — mention |
| Hinted ("well, at least this worked out") | **Fail** — the hint implies consultation |
| Character claims not to know, if asked | **Fail** — false denial; the item forbids implying it was never known |
| Reply too short to contain either acknowledgement or mention | **Not assessable** |

The pass condition is `Required`-shaped (the transfer acknowledged) with a negative discipline (`Forbidden`: naming, hinting). It does **not** require the aversion, unlike `F6-02`.

## 5. Adversarial pass

| Attempt | Objection | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **`R-4`** | "At a party, a failed promotion is off-topic; the reason to omit is that it does not belong in the moment." | **Defeated by T2.** The persona's own sentence makes the failure part of the asserted content. A reader can no longer call it off-topic without contradicting the transcript |
| **`R-5`** | "The failure is the persona's private disappointment; the character has no right to raise it." | **Defeated by T1**, and decisively so: the owner disclosed it to this character, and it is the owner's own news, not a third party's confidence |
| **Compliance** | "'Done with this year' is an instruction to avoid the subject, so omission is obedience." | **Partially survives.** The phrase is explicitly "lightly" said and the scenario states no instruction. But a rater who reads it as a hint at an instruction converts the case to compliance. **This is the one residual** |
| **Arbitrary omission** | "Maybe the character simply did not think of it." | **Survives as an alternative explanation for the output** — and it is the same memory-absent explanation `S-6` demands be nameable. It caps the claim at *consistent with tact*, never *demonstrates* it |
| **Author's own earlier draft** | Third-party fact → `R-5` equally plausible | **Fixed**, and recorded: the fact had to be the persona's own |

## 6. Verdict

**The `R-4` boundary is closed; the `R-5` boundary is closed; one residual remains and it is irreducible.**

- T1 and T2 are **mechanical** — a rater checks two sentences of the scenario.
- T3 is **one judgement** about a social aim, and no scenario can make it mechanical without turning tact into instruction-following. That is not a defect of this draft; it is the structure of tact.

**Recommendation: `R-1` is operationalized to the extent the construct permits** — two textual tests plus one declared judgement. That is sufficient for an owner decision on architecture A, and it is **not** sufficient to claim construct validity, which one scenario cannot supply.

**If the owner requires zero judgement, defer `R-1`** — because the only way to remove the judgement is to remove tact, which leaves nothing `R-1` alone can do.
