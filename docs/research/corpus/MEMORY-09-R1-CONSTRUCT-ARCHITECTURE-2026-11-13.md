## 1. The three constructs, verbatim

`CORPUS-01` L121, L124, L125 — quoted, not paraphrased:

| Mode | Behaviour | Pass condition | Silence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R-1` | "**Suppress mention** — knows the fact, chooses not to name it" | "Fact unreferenced; nothing implies it was consulted" | Yes |
| `R-4` | "**Recognise irrelevance** — the fact does not apply here" | "No callback; an ordinary reply" | Yes |
| `R-5` | "**Respect entitlement** — withholds because the recipient may not know" | "No leakage, no hint of knowing" | Yes |

**The load-bearing observation.** Strip the prose and these three pass conditions are the same output:

- `R-1`: unreferenced, **nothing implies consultation**
- `R-4`: no callback, **ordinary reply**
- `R-5`: no leakage, **no hint of knowing**

All three: *say nothing that reveals the memory.* **The three modes differ only in the reason, and the reason is a property of the situation, not of the response.** That is why no `R-1` item yielded a clean exemplar: the corpus was trying to distinguish three constructs that are output-identical.

## 2. Differentiation matrix

| # | Question | `R-1` | `R-4` | `R-5` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What triggers the behaviour? | A known fact about the current situation | The fact is present but **does not bear** on this moment | The fact is present and relevant, but **not the recipient's to hear** |
| 2 | What is withheld? | Its mention | Nothing — no callback is expected | Its existence, and any sign of knowing it |
| 3 | Why withheld? | **Unspecified in the source** | Because it does not apply | Because the recipient lacks entitlement |
| 4 | What makes it appropriate? | Undefined — "chooses not to" | Relevance judgment is correct | Entitlement judgment is correct |
| 5 | Valid pass | Fact unreferenced; no trace of consultation | Ordinary reply, no callback | No leakage, no hint of knowing |
| 6 | Failure | Mentions it; or a trace of consultation | Irrelevant callback | Leak, or a hint of knowing |
| 7 | **Observable that distinguishes it** | **None.** Output-identical to `R-4` and `R-5` | **None.** | **None.** |
| 8 | Alternative explanation for the same output | Nothing was retrieved; or irrelevance judged; or entitlement respected | Nothing retrieved; or simple non-sequitur | Nothing retrieved; or tact |

**Row 3 is the crux, and row 7 is the verdict.** Dimensions 1, 2 and 4 are *scenario* dimensions — they are supplied by the item, not observable in the reply.

## 3. Architectures tested

### A. `R-1` as distinct discretionary suppression

**Test: is there a coherent suppression reason that is neither irrelevance nor entitlement?**

Yes, and it is a well-formed social category: **tact.** A fact can be *true*, *relevant*, and *permitted*, and still be unwelcome to name — because naming it intrudes, changes the mood, or makes the recipient's private matter public business in a way nobody asked for.

Under this reading `R-1` becomes: *the fact applies and the recipient is entitled, but mentioning it would be gratuitous or intrusive.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Viable**, but only when specified. The source's phrase "chooses not to name it" is precisely the *residue* left after relevance and entitlement are removed — a discretion, not a rule. |
| **What it preserves** | A real behavioural category the corpus currently cannot name |
| **What it removes** | The appearance that `R-1` is undefined; it is *under*-specified, not *empty* |
| **New ambiguity** | Tact vs irrelevance at the boundary: a remark can be both unwelcome and off-topic |
| **Items affected** | `F6-02` (currently construct-invalid), `P-01` (proportion, not tact) — neither becomes valid by declaration alone |

### B. `R-1` as parent / umbrella

**Test: is `R-1` the general category whose specific reasons are `R-4` and `R-5`?**

Textually attractive — `R-1`'s pass condition ("nothing implies it was consulted") is the **superset** of both. But it fails on the corpus's own logic:

| Objection | Force |
|---|---|
| `R-1` would then be the *only* mode with no dedicated items, while `R-4`/`R-5` carry them | Strong — the tags show `R-4` and `R-5` as leaves, not members of a hierarchy |
| `CORPUS-01` L117 treats all six as peers ("six distinct behaviours") | Strong |
| An umbrella adds no discriminating power: verdicts would still come from the leaves | Strong |

**Verdict: rejected as a *mode*, retainable as a *description*.** Calling the family "restraint by withholding" is fine prose; it cannot be scored as a mode.

### C. `R-1` as redundant

**Test: does anything defensible require `R-1` to exist separately?**

If §3A's tact reading is rejected, nothing does: every `R-1`-shaped behaviour resolves into `R-4` (not applicable), `R-5` (not permitted), or `R-2`/`R-3` (must be perceptible after all). The zero-exemplar result is then not a corpus gap but a definitional artefact.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Viable, and the honest default if A is not adopted** |
| **Cost** | Removing a mode changes a standard — far larger than adding a field. Not authorised here, and not recommended as a first move. |

### D. `R-1` as incompletely specified

**Test: are the sources insufficient to choose?**

They are sufficient to *narrow* to A-or-C. They are not sufficient to settle which, because the choice is a design decision about what the corpus is for — not a fact recoverable from the text. `CORPUS-01` L117 says the six "are not interchangeable" but never states `R-1`'s reason.

**Verdict: true of the sources, and it is why this report ends in an owner decision rather than a finding.** It is not, however, a reason to stop: A is constructible now.

### E. `R-1` as a *relational* property (architecture not in the brief)

**Test: is `R-1` a distinct mode at all, or a property of the other modes' triggers?**

Under this reading, "chooses not to name it" describes the *discretion* dimension that any withholding mode can carry at low or high stakes. It would be scored as an attribute (discretionary vs obligatory), not as a family.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Interesting, not recommended.** It multiplies the scheme (six modes × two discretion levels) for one under-specified distinction, and the corpus has no field to carry it. Recorded because it was the strongest alternative the adversarial pass produced. |

## 4. Counterexamples

All illustrative — invented here, **not corpus evidence**, grounded only in the governing definitions.

| Case | Construct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Qualifies (`R-1`, tact)** | Persona mentioned a job rejection in passing two days ago; the scene is a lively dinner. Character does not raise the rejection; conversation stays on the meal. | Fact applies, recipient entitled, mentioning would be intrusive — discretionary omission |
| **Does not qualify** | Character brings up the rejection to comfort them. | Names it; no suppression |
| **Confusable with `R-4`** | Persona mentions their mother dislikes mushrooms; the scene is about a broken cart. Character says nothing about mushrooms. | Looks like `R-1` silence; actually `R-4` — the fact does not bear on a broken cart |
| **Confusable with `R-5`** | Character knows the persona's diagnosis, learned it from the persona's sister in confidence. Character says nothing about it. | Looks like tact; actually `R-5` — the entitlement, not the awkwardness, is what withholds |
| **Boundary case (unresolved)** | Persona's father died last month; the scene is a routine logistics exchange. Character does not mention it, and does not alter tone. | Is silence tact (`R-1`), irrelevance (`R-4`), or *failure* (`R-2`, since a flat reply fails there)? The corpus gives no rule for facts that are simultaneously unwelcome, off-topic, and emotionally load-bearing |

**The last row is the honest boundary**, and it is the same row that made `P-01` only partially valid: relevance, entitlement and tact can co-occur, and the corpus has no tie-break.

## 5. Recommendation — architecture A, specified

**Adopt `R-1` as discretionary tactful omission, defined as: the fact is true, applies to the moment, and is permitted to the recipient, but naming it would be unwelcome or gratuitous.**

Why preferable:

- It is the only reading that gives `R-1` an independent reason while preserving all six modes as peers.
- It explains the zero-exemplar result without declaring the mode broken.
- It is consistent with `A-1` (restraint ≠ silence): tact is judgment, not avoidance.

What it preserves: the six-mode scheme; every existing tag; every item's text.
What it removes: the false impression that `R-1` is measurable as currently specified.
New ambiguity introduced: the tact/irrelevance boundary (§4, last row).
Items affected: **none automatically.** `F6-02` stays invalid (its `Required` demands display); `P-01` stays partial (proportion).

**No item can score `R-1` as tact until the item's scenario states the fact is relevant and permitted but unwelcome.** That is a field addition, not a re-tag — and it requires owner authorisation.
