## 1. The defect

`CORPUS-87` §5 stated:

> The `CORPUS-81`-author exclusion is an **additional assignment restriction imposed by the owner**, *not* a requirement of `CORPUS-80` — see `CORPUS-87` §5.

And `CORPUS-85`/`CORPUS-86` §1 carried the same attribution in their inserted paragraph.

**The owner did say something, and it must be quoted rather than paraphrased.** From the role table assigning this administrative work:

> **Glyph Cluster** | Preserve `CORPUS-81` as its completed Q1 contribution | It already performed the Q1 examination. **Don't ask it to adjudicate its own work again.**

That instruction is explicit and I am not free to pretend it does not exist. **But its scope is narrow**: it is a statement about Glyph Cluster's own work — the Q1 contribution — and it sits in a table whose subject is *preserving that contribution*. It is **not** a statement that the `CORPUS-81` author is barred from Q2 and Q3.

**The owner's later note confirms that narrower scope**, and does so in terms that decide this correction:

> Excluding the CORPUS-81 author from Q2/Q3 **may be a sensible additional independence safeguard**, but it should be identified as **an additional assignment restriction, not presented as though CORPUS-80 explicitly required it**.

"May be sensible" is not an exercise of authority. It is an assessment of a safeguard.

**So the chain is:** owner instruction → *do not have Glyph Cluster re-adjudicate its own Q1 work* → **secretary's extension** → *therefore exclude that author from Q2 and Q3*. The extension is reasonable and I still recommend it. It is **mine**, and `CORPUS-87` §5 attributed it to the owner.

**Governing text, item adjudication, owner decision, secretary's extension** — the fourth label is the one that was missing, and its absence is the whole defect.

## 3. Why this matters more than the size of the error suggests

`CORPUS-87` was written to correct a secretary who recorded the owner's authorization as already given. In the same document, §5 recorded the secretary's own safeguard as an owner's decision.

That is the same class of error with the polarity reversed: the first inflated the owner's act into an accomplished fact; the second inflated the secretary's judgement into the owner's act. Both make the record claim an authority that was not exercised. That the second was small, sensible, and probably what the owner would have wanted is exactly why it would have survived unnoticed — a fabricated owner decision that nobody objects to is more durable than one that provokes a correction.

**Rule adopted, and binding on this secretary's later filings:** when the prepared paperwork adds anything to what the owner actually said, the addition is labelled **secretary's extension** with its reasoning, and the owner's narrower instruction is quoted beside it. A recommendation does not become a decision by being written into an instruction that the owner asked to be prepared.

## 4. What does not change

- **No practical restriction is altered in effect.** `CORPUS-80` is unissued (`CORPUS-87` §2), so `CORPUS-85`/`CORPUS-86` bind nobody. The three bars constrain nothing today; the correction is about who is entitled to impose them once issuance occurs.
- **The recommendation stands, as a recommendation.** Barring the `CORPUS-81` author from Q2/Q3 remains advisable — a reviewer should not build on its own earlier adjudication — and it is now labelled as advice rather than as an inherited rule.
- `CORPUS-84` and `CORPUS-87` are retained unedited. Their hashes remain valid for the files as filed, and the errors remain part of the record.
- No finding, proposal, standard, tag, split, threshold, or score is changed. `CORPUS-81` is untouched. No `CORPUS-78` decision is revived.
