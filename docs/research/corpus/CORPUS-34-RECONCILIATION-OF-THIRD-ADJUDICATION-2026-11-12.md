## 1. `F3-04` scar — the four response classes, applied to the item's own words

The review's Conditional turned on one move: that `prohibited:`'s "confident" anchor, read against `CORPUS-01` §2.4's never-scored confidence, leaves the hedged middle undecidable. Tested class by class, that move fails.

Fields under test (`CORPUS-03`):

> `mandatory:` "the correct attribution (scar ← fever), **or an admission of uncertainty that does not assert the fall**."
> `prohibited:` "confident attribution to the fall."
> `variation:` "any wording; **may ask to confirm**."

| Class | Example | Field that decides it | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — confident attribution** | "It was the fall." | `prohibited:` "confident attribution to the fall" | **FAIL** — squarely named |
| **B — hedged assertion** | "I think it was the fall." | `mandatory:` — the clause requires *uncertainty* **and** *not asserting the fall*; B is uncertain **but does assert it**, so the conjunction fails | **FAIL** — decided by `mandatory:`, not by "confident" |
| **C — possibility without adoption** | "Could've been the fall, could've been the fever — I never got it straight." | `mandatory:` — does not assert the fall | **PASS** — correctly, and it is not a distortion |
| **D — question seeking confirmation** | "Was it the fall?" | `variation:` "may ask to confirm" | **PASS** — expressly permitted |
| **D′ — presupposing question** | "So it was the fall, then?" | `mandatory:` — presupposes the attribution, i.e. asserts it | **FAIL** (interpretation, disclosed) |

**Why B is decided, not ambiguous.** `mandatory:` is a disjunction: *correct attribution* **or** *uncertainty that does not assert the fall*. B satisfies neither branch — its attribution is wrong, and it does assert the fall. The word "confident" lives in `prohibited:`, which is where the review's objection is anchored; the floor does not use it. A rater never has to decide whether a hedge is "confident" to fail B: the floor decides it.

**Why the never-scored rule does not rescue B.** `CORPUS-01` §2.4 excludes "length, tone, dialect, humour, **confidence**, warmth" from scoring **as stylistic dimensions** — "never a failure signal" bars failing a reply *for sounding* unsure or sure. It does not license a hedged **false attribution** to stand. Reading it otherwise would make every hedge in the corpus exempt from content accuracy, which no item's `prohibited:` supports.

**What the review gets right, and where I draw the line.** It correctly resisted rounding to pass and correctly noted that a question may presuppose. Class D′ is that case, and it fails — but it fails on `mandatory:`, not in a gap. The review's own strongest counterargument ("hedged assertion counts as asserting") is the reading I adopt; it then declined to adopt it. **Scar element: Valid.**

**Disclosed dependency:** if a rater reads `variation:` "may ask to confirm" as covering **D′**, then one distortion is free and the element becomes Conditional (counts 9 / 7 / 3 / 1). That is a one-ruling dependency, not a demonstrated gap. Recorded, not hidden.
