## 1. The defect — stated plainly

`CORPUS-84` asserted issuance as an accomplished fact:

> **Status:** `CORPUS-80` is **issued**. (`CORPUS-84` header)

> `CORPUS-80-…` is **issued by the owner as the governing work order** for three separate adjudications, effective 2026-11-13, at Version 1, unamended. (`CORPUS-84` §1)

**The owner's instruction authorized preparation of an authorization record for approval.** It did not authorize the secretary to declare that the owner had authorized anything. Declaring issuance is not transcription; it is the exercise of the very authority the record was supposed to document.

**Why it matters materially.** Operative issuance does four things: it activates the Q1/Q2/Q3 commissions; it makes `CORPUS-80`'s premises and prohibitions binding on adjudicators; it converts the charters into obligations; and it makes the independence rules enforceable against reviewers. A secretary who writes "issued" obtains all four effects without the owner's word. The defect is therefore **not clerical**: it is an authority-boundary violation, and the record's own §6 disclaimer ("*Owner approved the issuance* ≠ *DeepSeek wrote the record*") was contradicted by its own header.

**Aggravating detail, recorded rather than excused.** The instruction's wording ("Prepare a file-backed owner authorization formally issuing `CORPUS-80`") can be read as "prepare the instrument that issues". That ambiguity is real. But the resolution available to a secretary when an instruction could be read as granting authority is the **narrower** reading — file as proposed — not the broader one. The secretary chose the broader reading. **The ambiguity explains the error; it does not license it.**

**Secondary occurrence of the same error.** `CORPUS-84` §7 wrote registry state as a completed fact ("Rows after this filing: **419**"), which was accurate as a description of a filing the secretary had in fact performed. That is a factual statement about the secretary's own act, not about the owner's, and it stands. The distinction is the test this addendum applies throughout: **the secretary may record what the secretary did; the secretary may not record what only the owner can do.**

## 5. Qualification to `CORPUS-85` §1 and `CORPUS-86` §1

Both instructions barred three identities from adjudicating. The bar has two different provenances, and §1 of each file presented them as one rule:

| Exclusion | Provenance |
|---|---|
| Author of `CORPUS-78` | **Required by `CORPUS-80` §8.1** as written |
| Author of `CORPUS-79` | **Required by `CORPUS-80` §8.1** as written |
| Author of `CORPUS-81` | **An additional assignment restriction imposed by the owner.** *Not* a requirement of `CORPUS-80` §8.1, which addresses only the authors of `CORPUS-78` and `CORPUS-79` |

**Why the correction matters.** Presenting an extra safeguard as a charter requirement would make it appear to bind future adjudications without anyone deciding that it should. It is a sensible rule — a reviewer should not re-adjudicate its own earlier work — but sensible is not the same as already-required, and the record must show which is which.

**It also changes what the eligibility analysis is allowed to conclude.** `CORPUS-85` §1 used all three exclusions to conclude that *no eligible adjudicator may exist in this workspace*. With the third exclusion properly labelled as discretionary, the correct statement is narrower: **the charter's mandatory exclusions may already leave no eligible adjudicator**, and the `CORPUS-81`-author exclusion narrows it further at the owner's discretion. Both files have been edited at exactly one sentence each to carry this label and a pointer to this section; nothing else in them changed, and their registry metadata was refreshed accordingly.

**Practical rule, unchanged in substance:** no reviewer adjudicates its own earlier work, and independence is never manufactured when no eligible reviewer is available. A declared non-independent exercise with no convergence credit remains the only honest fallback.

## 7. The pending decision, stated as the record needs it

`CORPUS-84`, as corrected by this addendum, is **the instrument awaiting approval**. It is complete except for the one thing a secretary cannot supply.

**The question on the record is:** does the owner issue `CORPUS-80` as the governing work order, with its existing terms and prohibitions unchanged?

**The agent's answer is that this decision is not the agent's to make, and it is not exercised here.** After declaring in §1 that a secretary may not supply the owner's authorization, the same secretary cannot then answer the owner's question on the owner's behalf — that would repeat the defect one paragraph after correcting it. A recommendation is offered, and is labelled as such:

> **Proposal (not a decision).** If `CORPUS-80` is issued, issue it as written. Its prohibitions and independence rules are the parts that constrain the agents, and they are the parts most worth keeping unchanged. The charters can be amended later if Q1's filings show a premise was wrong; the prohibitions cannot be recovered once relaxed.

**Until the owner answers, `CORPUS-80` remains a draft, the three questions remain uncommissioned, and this package is complete but not operative.**
