## 1. The check, and the test it applies

**D5A-01 (Observed — the task).** A bounded check of DQ-5 against the settled DQ-2 and DQ-3 records: does it still
contain a decision, or has its subject gone?

**D5A-02 (Calculated — what makes a decision "live").** A question has a live owner decision when three things hold:
**(a)** it has a **subject** — an obligation or arrangement it would govern; **(b)** it has an **answer space** — more than
one outcome remains available; **(c)** its answer would have a **consequence** — something would change, be stated, or be
ruled out. If any fails, the question has no decision to take, even if its wording survives.

**D5A-03 (Observed — why the wording can outlive the subject).** DQ-5 was written when retention was undecided (GRP-53).
Two decisions have since fixed what is retained and what is required (DQD-04; D3D-04). A scope term whose object no longer
exists is not thereby answered; it is **without a subject**, which is a different finding and must be recorded as such.

**D5A-04 (Calculated — no new decision may be manufactured).** If a residual question emerges, this record names it and
stops: naming is not opening (ODC-31's discipline), and a scope term may not be stretched to cover a question it does not
ask.

**D5A-05 (Observed — what stays out).** ND-1, DQ-4, the prohibition question, QAD-19, the report-only boundary and axis H
are untouched. The time asymmetry of GRP-36 is used as evidence, not as an argument (GRP-36; ODL-17).

## 2. What DQ-5 asked, and what has changed

**D5A-06 (Source-derived — the question).** "Does retention apply **prospectively only**, and is that stated?" Its
dependency is "GRP-36's time asymmetry", and its "cannot borrow from" is "nothing — this is a scope term of the decision
itself" (GRP-53).

**D5A-07 (Observed — its earlier narrowing).** With DQ-2 adding nothing, the only retention obligation in force is D-3b,
an existing obligation applying to events as they occur; DQ-5's present subject was narrowed to D-3b and its remaining
content left unestablished (DPD-17; DSC-14).

**D5A-08 (Source-derived — DQ-2).** **No ground-retention requirement beyond D-3b**; P-2 inapplicable, so no coverage
class was fixed; **descriptive baseline** (DQD-04, DQD-08, DQD-07). No new retention obligation exists whose temporal
scope DQ-5 could fix.

**D5A-09 (Source-derived — DQ-3).** **A = yes**: a recorded rationale must be preserved faithfully and attributed
(D3D-04, D3D-05). **B = no**: no general prohibition on evaluation (D3D-11). A is a **representation** requirement,
conditional on a record existing (D3D-07); it is not a retention obligation.

**D5A-10 (Calculated — neither decision supplies DQ-5 a subject).** DQ-2 removed the only possible object of a new scope
term. DQ-3 added a constraint on **how** an existing record is preserved — not on **which** records exist — so it creates
no retention to scope. DQ-5's subject is therefore whatever D-3b already covers, and nothing else.

## 4. Reading (ii) — post-hoc completion

**D5A-16 (Calculated — what the reading would ask, in two parts).** **(a)** May a basis that was not recorded at write
time be supplied later at all? **(b)** If it may, must it be marked as a later statement, so it is not read as
contemporaneous?

**D5A-17 (Observed — (a) is the corpus's state, not a decision waiting to be taken).** Nothing forbids recording a basis
(GRP-27; PGM-29), and nothing requires completing one (DQD-04). So "may it be supplied later" is answered by the absence
of a prohibition — the same absence DQD-13 recorded — and no obligation attaches to either answer. There is no choice
between two outcomes; there is one state.

**D5A-18 (Calculated — and the answer does not depend on DQ-5).** Whether a later statement may be recorded is a
consequence of there being no prohibition, not of a scope term. A scope term cannot create or remove a prohibition
(DQD-13), so reading (ii)(a) is not something DQ-5 could settle even if it had a subject.

**D5A-19 (Calculated — (b) is not asked by DQ-5's wording).** DQ-5 asks about the **temporal scope of retention**, not
about **temporal provenance in the record**. "Must a later statement be marked as later" is a representation/recording
question, of the same family as DQ-3's A — and A does not settle it: A requires faithful substance and attribution
(D3D-04), and says nothing about timestamps or about distinguishing a later statement from a contemporaneous one. The
scope term cannot be stretched to cover it (D5A-04).

**D5A-20 (Unestablished — and (b) would need a determination this record does not make).** Whether a genuine later
recollection is inside or outside M-3's prohibition on fabrication is itself undetermined (DSC-18; D5A-14). So even the
residual question rests on an unresolved distinction — which is a further reason not to fold it into DQ-5.

**D5A-21 (Calculated — so reading (ii) has no live decision either).** Its first part is a state, not a choice; its second
part is a different question that DQ-5 does not ask. **No live owner decision remains in reading (ii) as scoped.**

**D5A-22 (Observed — the asymmetry GRP-36 recorded is unaffected).** "A basis written after the fact is not a record of a
contemporaneous claim" remains a fact about when a cost falls, and the omission is still paid at write time (GRP-36;
PGM-26). Nothing in this finding changes that; it only removes it as a decision's subject.
