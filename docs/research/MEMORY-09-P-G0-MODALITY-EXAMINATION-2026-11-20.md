## 1. The question and its three readings

**PGM-01 (Observed — the question).** Whether P-G0's "no ground" is **normative** — a rule forbidding the retention of
a basis — or **descriptive** — a statement that no basis is required, and so none is retained. The owner's framing:
"Does this actually prohibit retaining additional grounds, or does it merely describe what is currently required?"

**PGM-02 (Observed — why it now matters).** Post-Q-α, P-G0 and P-G1 retain identical content (PGA-13/PGA-14). The
prohibition reading is the **only** reading on which P-G0 remains distinct from P-G1 at write time (PGA-24/PGA-33).
So this question carries the whole of P-G0's remaining distinctness.

**PGM-03 (Calculated — the question has three readings, and only two were on the table).**

| Reading | What P-G0 is | Distinct from P-G1? |
|---|---|---|
| **(i) descriptive silence** | a statement that nothing requires a basis, so none appears | **no** — identical (PGA-13) |
| **(ii) writer prohibition** | a rule that a transition must not carry a basis beyond D-3b | **yes** — restrictive at write time |
| **(iii) decision-level foreclosure** | the answer "no ground is required" to DQ-2, closing the question rather than constraining writers | **no at write time**; differs only in whether DQ-2 stays open |

**PGM-04 (Inferred — reading (iii) is the one a "retention position" most naturally is).** GRP-53's DQ-2 asks "is a
ground required stream-wide, **and at which of P-G0/P-G1/P-G2**". The positions are answers to that question, so P-G0's
content is an **answer about requirements**, not automatically a constraint on writers. That framing does not by itself
make P-G0 normative at write time — it makes it a decision about what is required.

**PGM-05 (Observed — what would settle it).** P-G0's own statement, and the corpus's characterisations of it. Nothing
else can: the position was not drafted as normative text and has no canonical standing (PGM-07).

**PGM-06 (Observed — boundaries).** This examination does not select a position, does not create a prohibition, and
does not convert a reading into a requirement. If the owner intends P-G0 to be restrictive, that is a **choice to be
made**, not a reading to be discovered (§5, PGM-30).

## 2. What P-G0's own statement contains

**PGM-07 (Source-derived — where P-G0 comes from).** TGP-49, item 1, verbatim:

> "**No ground** — 'why' becomes unrecoverable; the stream stays cheapest and I17 cannot be violated by a ground
> because none exists."

**PGM-08 (Calculated — the statement is entirely descriptive).** Its three clauses state **consequences**: "why"
becomes unrecoverable; the stream stays cheapest; I17 cannot be violated by a ground because none exists. There is no
modal verb of obligation or prohibition anywhere in the item — no "must", "may not", "shall", "is forbidden". It
describes a resulting state, not a rule that produces it.

**PGM-09 (Source-derived — GRP-19's restatement, same shape).** "No transition retains any basis; 'why' becomes
unrecoverable; I17 cannot be violated by a ground because none exists." Again: a statement about what the stream
retains, phrased as an outcome. Notably, I17's non-violation is explained by **the absence of a ground**, not by a rule
against recording one — which is what a prohibition would have said.

**PGM-10 (Source-derived — GRP-25's writer obligation for P-G0).** "none regarding basis; M-2/M-3 unchanged." The
writer's obligation is stated as **none**. A prohibition is not "none"; it is a specific obligation of abstention, and
it would have been written as such.

**PGM-11 (Source-derived — GRP-29's characterisation).** "P-G0 is a **silence**; P-G1 is a decided floor; P-G2 is an
undecided addition." The word chosen was silence.

**PGM-12 (Source-derived — GRP-32's characterisation).** "P-G0's content is 'nothing', which is **not an obligation**
and therefore needs no decision — except for the one it would displace." An explicit denial that P-G0's content is an
obligation.

**PGM-13 (Source-derived — GRP-28's denial of forbidding).** "None of the three **requires, forbids or supplies**" the
listed items. The comparison's own summary attributes **no forbidding** to any position.

**PGM-14 (Calculated — the corpus's count).** Four characterisations of P-G0 — "none" (writer obligation), "a
silence", "not an obligation", "requires, forbids or supplies" — plus two descriptive statements of consequence. **All
point to (i) or (iii). None states a prohibition.**

## 3. The prohibition reading, and what supports it

**PGM-15 (Observed — the one phrase the reading rests on).** GRP-26: "P-G0 and P-G2 both **resolve** that region — by
**leaving it empty** and by filling it — while P-G1 leaves it **as it is today: ungoverned**."

**PGM-16 (Calculated — what that phrase does and does not say).** "Leaving it empty" describes the **outcome** for the
statement-bearing region: no basis is recorded there. It does not say a basis **may not** be recorded. "Resolve" is
ambiguous between *deciding that it stays empty* and *forbidding it from being filled* — and the sentence does not
choose.

**PGM-17 (Unestablished — no text supplies the prohibition).** No provision in r33, v9 or the decision records forbids
recording a basis on a lifecycle transition. GRP-27 found the converse: "None requires that a transition record its
basis, and **none forbids it**." A prohibition would therefore be a **new normative constraint**, not a description of
an existing one.

**PGM-18 (Narrowed — PGA-24's table overstated the reading).** PGA-24 recorded, for P-G0-b, "recording a basis would
violate the position". That was an inference from GRP-26's "leaving it empty" into a rule the position does not state.
On this examination the inference is **not supported** and PGA-24's P-G0-b row, together with PGA-30's "prohibit"
setting, is **narrowed**: it describes what a prohibition *would* consist of, not what P-G0 says.

**PGM-19 (Calculated — why the error was easy).** A position named "no ground" invites being read as a rule about
grounds. But a name is not a statement, and P-G0's statement (PGM-07) is about consequences. Reading the name as a rule
is the same species of move as reading absence of discussion as a boundary (QAX-18).

**PGM-20 (Calculated — what a prohibition would require to exist).** Three things, none present: a sentence forbidding
the recording; a statement of what the prohibition binds (writers? the fold? the schema?); and a decision that creates
it. GRP-28 puts a validity predicate, an authority condition, a closure declaration, an occurrence scheme, an identity
scheme, an evidence rule and any change to derived state in the same category — things **no position** requires,
forbids or supplies. A record-level prohibition would be a new item of that kind.

## 4. The owner's example, worked through

**PGM-21 (Source-derived — the case).** A character says: "I retracted that memory because I discovered new evidence."
Asked: does P-G0 forbid Howl from preserving that explanation, or does it simply not require Howl to preserve it?

**PGM-22 (Calculated — first, the transition is statement-bearing).** There **is** a source statement — the character's
utterance. So D-3b does not reach the transition (GRP-26): M-1 attaches to events **without** a source statement. The
example therefore lands squarely in the ungoverned region, which is exactly the region where the readings differ.

**PGM-23 (Calculated — second, "preserving the explanation" is two different things).** The example's answer depends on
which is meant, and they must be separated before any reading can be applied:

| What is preserved | What governs it | Effect of P-G0 |
|---|---|---|
| **the utterance itself** — the sentence exists in the conversation record | the acquisition/conversation provisions and their spans, not the retention axis | **none** — P-G0 speaks about whether a *transition* retains a basis, and says nothing about what the character said or whether it is kept |
| **the link** — the transition carries a resolvable reference to that statement, so a later reader recovers the stated basis without re-deriving it from present state (TGP-16) | the retention position | **on (i) and (iii): not required; on (ii): forbidden** |

**PGM-24 (Calculated — so the plain-English answer is: neither forbids the sentence, and only one forbids the link).**
P-G0 does not forbid preserving the explanation in the sense of the words remaining in the conversation; that was never
within its scope. Under readings (i) and (iii) it also does not require the transition to point at them — the cost is
that "why" must be re-derived from the conversation later, which is TGP-16's deficit and CLV-25's "loses the ground".
Only under (ii) would the transition be barred from carrying the link.

**PGM-25 (Inferred — the consequence for the owner's distinction).** The example shows the difference is narrower than
"restrictive vs permissive about information". Under (i)/(iii), nothing is destroyed and nothing is forbidden; what is
absent is the **reference**, and its absence is a real cost — auditability without re-derivation is lost (TGP-44/45;
B-5) — but it is a cost of omission, not of prohibition.

**PGM-26 (Calculated — and the cost is not recoverable by later decision).** GRP-36 stands: a basis written after the
fact is not a record of a contemporaneous claim, so the omission is paid at write time. That is a fact about when the
cost falls, explicitly **not** an argument for retaining (GRP-36; ODL-17).

**PGM-27 (Observed — no position implied).** Working the example does not indicate that P-G0 is wrong, that P-G1 is
right, or that the link should be required. It shows which reading each answer presupposes.

**PGM-31 (Observed — Q-β remains the only live difference, and it is not a difference between positions).** Q-β asks

P-G0's name says "no ground"; P-G1's name says "D-3b's floor". If Q-β says the rationale **is** a ground, P-G0's name
is inaccurate and P-G1's is accurate — a difference in description only, with identical content either way.

**PGM-32 (Calculated — the axis's settings, restated).** On the supported readings there are **two** settings:
**ungoverned** (the baseline, which both P-G0 and P-G1 name) and **require** (P-G2). A third — **prohibit** — exists

made.

**PGM-33 (Observed — nothing is selected or dropped).** P-G0 remains available: as the descriptive position, as the
