## 2. C-2 — the constraint package

**C23-04 (Source-derived — the candidate content).** C-2 was defined as "what constraints already apply, without
adding new ones" (NAB-37). The constraints in view are: the fold creates **no new event kind, field or key**; it

**C23-05 (Calculated — every clause is already in force).** Each item has an independent source: K-R12 for the

**C23-06 (Calculated — C-2 contains no substantive choice).** A decision requires at least two live alternatives. In
C-2, the alternatives are "restate the constraints" or "do not restate them." Both leave every constraint exactly as
it is. **C-2 is a consolidation, not a decision.**

**C23-07 (Calculated — and a "C-2 decision" would risk a false addition).** Recording already-binding constraints as
the product of a decision invites two errors: treating them as newly created (which would change the requirement
count or imply they were previously optional), and treating the act of recording as a licence to specify the fold.
The map must stay at **118**; DQ-3's requirement remains the acknowledged capture gap.

The choice among these is bookkeeping. It does not need the owner's normative authority, and it does not become one

**C23-09 (Observed — what C-2 must not do).** It must not: create an **authority rule** or permission model (C-1's

**C23-10 (Calculated — C-2's readiness verdict).** C-2 is **ready to be recorded** as a consolidation and **not ready

## 3. C-3 — whether N-a is status-only

**C23-11 (Observed — the question as defined).** C-3 was defined as "whether N-a's scope is status-only" (NAB-37),
against K-R12's statement that "a memory's current status, class and scope are a **projection of that stream**"
(`:111`).

**C23-12 (Calculated — three distinct meanings hide inside "status-only").**

| Meaning | What it asserts | What kind of question it is |
|---|---|---|
| **S-A — exclusive** | N-a covers **status and nothing else**; class and scope are outside N-a permanently | a **scope determination** |
| **S-B — deferred** | N-a covers status **now**; class and scope are separate parts to be opened later | a **sequencing determination** |
| **S-C — non-fold** | class and scope are **not folded at all**, in any part; the stream carries only status transitions | a **representation finding** about the architecture |

S-A and S-C can coincide in effect but differ in claim: S-A is about N-a's boundary, S-C is about the lifecycle
stream's vocabulary. A decision that answered one would not have answered the others.

**C23-13 (Source-derived — the evidence).** K-R12 names status, class and scope as projections of one stream
(`:111`). The named kinds — `established | superseded | corrected | retracted` — are status-shaped; class and scope
appear only in the phrase "plus `scope` and `knowledge_class` changes." Formation writes class and scope (`:604`;
`:404`; ECI-04/05), and the record carries them as fields (`:341`). **No kind names a class or scope change.**

**C23-14 (Calculated — the feasibility asymmetry).** A status-only fold is **statable over the existing kinds**. A
joint or independent fold over class and scope requires change events that do not exist, i.e. new kinds — excluded by
NSD-09 and A-6 (NAB-26). So one answer is expressible today and the others are not. That is a constraint on the
decision space, not a preference among its answers.

**C23-15 (Source-derived — the scope/domain caution).** r33's `scope` field (`world` / `scenario` /
`character_public` / `character_private`, §5 `:318`) is **not** v9's `domain` (the per-world/scenario
**authority** domain of O-8a). The conceptual-object investigation records them as **different axes** (RX-17).
C-3 must not read K-R12's "scope" as an authority scope, and must not import O-8a's domain into the lifecycle.

**C23-16 (Source-derived — the projection/field contradiction is already recorded).** K-R12 says status, class and
scope are a projection "not mutable authoritative fields"; §6.1 nevertheless lists `status` as a record field, and
`supersedes`/`superseded_by`/`correction_of` as relations **for which no event exists to project from** (A2-11).
A2-12: the lifecycle stream is "authoritative by declaration and unspecified by schema, while its data survives as
mutable record fields and relations." C-3 therefore cannot assume that class and scope are already modelled as
stream entries; the gap is the same one that affects status.

**C23-17 (Calculated — what C-3 would need to establish).**

| # | Term | Why it is required |
|---|---|---|
| **T-1** | **which meaning** — S-A, S-B or S-C | "status-only" currently names three different claims (C23-12) |
| **T-2** | whether class/scope changes are **intended as lifecycle entries at all** | K-R12 says they are stream inputs but names no kind; NAB-28 is Unestablished |
| **T-3** | whether the determination is about **N-a** or about the **whole stream's vocabulary** | S-A and S-C differ in subject |
| **T-4** | an explicit statement that it creates **no kind, field, key, requirement or schema** | otherwise the answer would need the very vocabulary it excludes |
| **T-5** | its relation to **N-b** (`provisional` representation) and to K-R12's three-dimension sentence | a status-only N-a does not settle either |
| **T-6** | what remains **open** — class/scope semantics, N-b, N-c, the entry architecture | stated, not implied |

**C23-18 (Calculated — what C-3 does not need).** It does not need a fold shape, an entry architecture, an authority
rule, a sufficiency standard, or a resolution of §11's adoption status. It is a scope question about **which
dimensions N-a covers**, not about how any of them is computed.

**C23-19 (Calculated — C-3's readiness verdict).** C-3 is **not ready as a binary**. It becomes ready when T-1 is
fixed, because S-A, S-B and S-C have different consequences. If the owner's answer is S-A or S-B, C-3 is a genuine
scope or sequencing decision. If it is S-C, C-3 is closer to a **finding** — class and scope cannot be folded without
vocabulary the constraints forbid — and the owner's act is to record the finding rather than to choose a scope. The
record does not close C-3 on that basis; it names the form the answer would take.

**C23-20 (Calculated — C-1's effect on C-3).** C-1 removed the authority reading of §11, so the domain question is now
about an **epistemic/representational** fold. That does not make class or scope authority-free everywhere: they remain
authority-bearing in v9's separate operation, and the scope/domain distinction (C23-15) must survive. It means only
that C-3 is not a question about who may act.

**C23-21 (Calculated — C-2 and C-3 are independent, with one-way pressure).** C-2 does not answer C-3, and C-3 does
not answer C-2. But C-2's "no new kinds" constraint is what makes C-3's asymmetry bite: without it, the class/scope
branches would be merely unwritten; with it, they are excluded. C-2 is therefore **context for** C-3, not a premise
of it.

**C23-22 (Observed — C-3 does not settle N-b).** `active`/`established` was absorbed into N-b, and `provisional`'s
representation remains unresolved (NSD-06, NSD-07, NSD-18). A status-only N-a still needs a status vocabulary to
fold; C-3 does not supply it.

**C23-23 (Calculated — C-3 does not settle N-c).** If the status fold is order-sensitive, N-c has a subject; if not,
it does not. C-3 fixes the **domain**, not the **shape**, so N-c's relevance still depends on N-a's eventual answer
(NSD-12).
