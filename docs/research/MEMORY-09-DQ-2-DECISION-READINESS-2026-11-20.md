## 1. The question, and why it is three questions

**DRD-01 (Source-derived — DQ-2 as the comparison posed it).** "**DQ-2** — Is a **ground** required stream-wide, and at
which of P-G0/P-G1/P-G2?" Its stated dependency is "the owner alone", and the comparison records what it cannot
borrow from: "I17 (which constrains interpretation, not retention — B-2)" (GRP-53).

**DRD-02 (Observed — the owner's three-part separation).** The decision to be prepared here keeps three questions
apart: whether a ground-retention requirement exists **beyond D-3b**; whether such a requirement applies to **every
lifecycle transition or only a defined subset**; and whether the answer is **descriptive baseline**, **additional
requirement**, or **separately authorised prohibition**. They are recorded as **P-1**, **P-2** and **P-3**.

**DRD-03 (Calculated — they are separable because they answer different things).** P-1 asks whether a requirement
exists; P-2 asks what a requirement ranges over; P-3 asks what **kind** of statement the answer is. A yes to P-1 does
not fix P-2, and neither fixes P-3: "a requirement exists covering every transition" and "a requirement exists
covering a subset" are both additions, while "no requirement exists, and this is a description of the baseline" and
"no requirement exists, and this is a prohibition" are both answers to P-1 that differ in kind.

**DRD-04 (Calculated — the questions are ordered only in one direction).** P-2 is **conditional** on P-1: if no
requirement exists beyond D-3b, there is no coverage to fix. P-3 is **not** conditional on P-1: a descriptive baseline
and a prohibition are both answers available when P-1 is answered "no". So the dependency runs P-1 → P-2, with P-3
cross-cutting both. **This ordering is analytic; it does not imply P-1 should be decided first, and this record does not
say it should** (GRP-54's discipline).

**DRD-05 (Calculated — "stream-wide" is itself an unfixed term).** GRP-53's wording admits at least three readings:
(a) every **lifecycle** transition; (b) both **streams**, acquisition and lifecycle, which Q-α put within D-3b's reach
(QAD-04); (c) every transition **not already covered** by D-3b — which is the statement-bearing region (GRP-26). Each
would give a different P-2 answer, and none is chosen here. The term is recorded as one a decision must fix (DRD-35),
not as a defect in the question.

**DRD-06 (Observed — what DQ-2 is not).** Not DQ-3 (whether a retained ground must be recorded **as stated and never
evaluated** — CLV-28's constraint), not DQ-4 (whether a **validity basis** is a separate obligation — ND-1's
neighbour), not DQ-5 (whether retention is **prospective only**), and not the prohibition option's own decision
(PGM-30). Those remain separate questions with their own records (GRP-53).

## 2. P-1 — is there a ground-retention requirement beyond D-3b?

**DRD-07 (Calculated — the distinction P-1 turns on).** *A ground being retained* and *a requirement to retain a
ground* are different facts. Post-Q-β the baseline retains a ground wherever the D-3b cause is known only from the
actor's account (QBD-14) — and it does so **because D-3b requires the cause**, not because any provision requires a
ground. So "a ground exists in the stream" does not entail "a ground-retention requirement exists".

**DRD-08 (Observed — what the corpus says about requirements beyond D-3b).** GRP-27: "**None requires that a
transition record its basis**, and none forbids it", checked against K-R12's transition kinds (`:111`), §11's promotion
conditions (`:611`–`:613`) and §11's glosses on the exit kinds (`:606`–`:609`). GRP-33 repeats it for the stream-wide
obligation; GRP-28 lists what no position requires, forbids or supplies.

**DRD-09 (Calculated — the answer space for P-1).**

| Answer | What it asserts | Consequence of the assertion |
|---|---|---|
| **no** | nothing beyond D-3b's cause obligation requires a basis to be retained | the baseline is correctly described as "no ground requirement"; the ground that exists is a classification of D-3b's artifact (QBD-14) |
| **yes** | some requirement beyond D-3b requires a retained basis | the requirement set grows by one obligation no current provision states (GRP-27); P-2 becomes operative and must be answered (DRD-13) |

**DRD-10 (Calculated — a "yes" is new normative content, whichever way it is phrased).** P-G2 is the only position in
the option space that adds an obligation (GRP-27; PGR-30), and GRP-22 records why it is not "P-G1 everywhere": it
changes the **kind** of obligation, from a per-event cause rule to a per-transition basis record. A "yes" that covered
only a subset would be an addition too — no text supplies a basis requirement for the statement-bearing region either
(GRP-26). So P-1's "yes" is not made cheaper by narrowing its coverage; it remains an addition whose provenance is the
owner's decision.

**DRD-11 (Observed — P-1 does not ask whether the existing ground is worth having).** The ground retained today is
self-reported testimony that "can raise auditability… but cannot by itself raise reliability" (TGP-20; GRP-09), and
retention does not make validity decidable (GRP-35; TGP-44). Those are properties of the content, recorded elsewhere
and **not** weighed here. P-1 asks only whether a requirement exists.

**DRD-12 (Calculated — and P-1's "no" is not a prohibition).** An answer of "no" states that nothing requires a
retained basis beyond D-3b. It does not state that one **may not** be retained: P-G0's statement contains no modal verb
of obligation or prohibition, and its writer obligation is recorded as "none" (PGM-08, PGM-10, PGM-29). A prohibition is
P-3's third branch and needs its own authority (PGM-30).

## 3. P-2 — every lifecycle transition, or a defined subset?

**DRD-13 (Calculated — P-2 is operative only on a "yes" to P-1).** If P-1 is answered "no", P-2 has nothing to range
over; the record still needs P-2's term stated so that "no requirement" is not read as "no requirement on some
transitions" (DRD-35, T-2). If P-1 is answered "yes", P-2 must name a class, and the class cannot be left implicit.

**DRD-14 (Calculated — the candidate classes, listed without ranking).**

| Class | Its state | Why it is a candidate |
|---|---|---|
| **every lifecycle transition** | K-R12 enumerates the kinds (`:111`); no provision requires a basis for any of them | P-G2's scope as stated: "a stated basis on every transition" (GRP-25) |
| **the statement-bearing region** | ungoverned today; its size unestablished (GRP-26) | the region D-3b does not reach, and the only place a new requirement would add coverage rather than duplicate D-3b |
| **the report-only subset** | boundary undrawn (CGE-29/CGE-30) | where a ground is already retained by classification (QBD-14) — a requirement here would be least additive in content |
| **particular transition kinds** | the kinds exist (entry, supersession, correction, retraction, forgetting — TGP-10/TGP-11) | a per-kind rule is available in principle; nothing states one |

**DRD-15 (Calculated — any subset answer requires a boundary that is not currently drawn).** The two boundaries that
would define the natural subsets are both undrawn: **which transitions are statement-less** (QAD-19; TGP-11) and **where
a cause is known only by report** (CGE-29/CGE-30). A decision that named either as its coverage would be drawing that
boundary by implication — which QAD-19 expressly is not authorised to do and which this record does not do.

**DRD-16 (Observed — D-3b's class cannot be inherited without a decision).** D-3b's obligation is per-event, not
per-stream: "the obligation is per-event, not per-stream" (TGP-11; QAD-19). A new requirement "where D-3b applies"
would therefore inherit an undrawn boundary, and its coverage would be as indeterminate as the boundary is. Stated as a
property of the option, not as an objection to it.

**DRD-17 (Calculated — the coverage term is what distinguishes "more of the same" from "a different obligation").**
GRP-22: P-G2 changes the kind of obligation, not just its coverage. If P-2 named **every** transition, the requirement
would cover the statement-bearing region as well and would be a per-transition basis record; if it named the
**statement-bearing region only**, it would be an addition of a different shape. Neither is more consistent with the
corpus, because the corpus states neither.

**DRD-18 (Observed — coverage is not a proxy for importance).** GRP-36's time asymmetry (a basis written after the fact
is not a contemporaneous claim) is a fact about **when a cost falls**, expressly not an argument for retaining
(GRP-36; ODL-17; PGM-26). It bears on P-2 only in that a narrower class narrows the write-time surface; it does not
rank the classes.

**DRD-19 (Unestablished — the size of every candidate class).** No class on DRD-14's list has a measured extent: the
statement-bearing region's size is unestablished (GRP-26), the statement-less boundary is undrawn (QAD-19), and the
report-only subset is unmeasured (CGE-29/30; QBD-21). So P-2 can be answered as a **class**, not as a **quantity**, and
this record does not estimate one.

## 4. P-3 — descriptive baseline, additional requirement, or prohibition?

**DRD-20 (Calculated — the three kinds are not three positions on one axis).** They answer "what sort of statement is
this decision making?", not "how much should be retained?":

| Kind | What it does | Does the requirement set change? |
|---|---|---|
| **descriptive baseline** | states what the present arrangement requires and retains — no normative change | **no** |
| **additional requirement** | adds an obligation that no provision currently states | **yes** — a new requirement exists (GRP-27) |
| **separately authorised prohibition** | forbids retaining a basis beyond D-3b | **yes** — a new normative constraint, of the forbidding kind (PGM-30) |

**DRD-21 (Calculated — how the three retention positions map onto the three kinds, stated as description only).**
P-G1's content is the **descriptive baseline**: D-3b applied as written, no requirement added (PGR-12; GRP-25). P-G2's
content is the **additional requirement** (GRP-22; GRP-27). **P-G0 is neither**: its statement contains no prohibition
(PGM-29), and its "no ground" description is now inaccurate over the report-only subset (QBD-17) — so P-G0 is a
descriptive claim whose accuracy has changed, and it becomes a prohibition **only if the owner creates one** (PGM-30).
This mapping is a description of what each position asserts; it selects, ranks and removes none.

**DRD-22 (Observed — P-3's "descriptive baseline" is no longer "no ground anywhere").** Post-Q-β the accurate
descriptive statement is: D-3b's cause is retained where the event is statement-less, and **where that cause is
report-only it is also a ground** (QBD-05, QBD-14). A descriptive-baseline answer therefore does **not** say the stream
carries no ground; it says no ground **requirement** exists beyond D-3b.

**DRD-23 (Calculated — the three kinds are mutually exclusive, and mixing them is the failure mode).** A decision that
answered "descriptive baseline" while also forbidding retention would be a prohibition in descriptive clothing; one that
answered "additional requirement" while denying it adds a requirement would be a description in requirement clothing.
Each kind's consequence in DRD-20 is what distinguishes it, and exactly one may be chosen.

**DRD-24 (Observed — the prohibition cannot be read out of the baseline).** P-G0's own statement and every
characterisation of it describe an outcome; "leaving it empty" describes what happens, not what is forbidden (PGM-16,
PGM-29). If a prohibition is wanted, it is P-3's third branch and requires its own explicit decision and record
(PGM-30) — it cannot be obtained by interpreting the baseline, and "no requirement" is not a prohibition (DRD-12).

**DRD-25 (Calculated — the prohibition's subject would also have to be fixed).** A prohibition is not self-specifying:
PGM-20 records that it would need a sentence forbidding the recording, a statement of whom it binds (writers, the fold,
the schema) and a decision creating it. That is a term of P-3's third branch, not of this record, and it is listed in
DRD-35 so the branch is not answered by a phrase.

## 5. What Q-β does not answer — the three boundaries the owner named

**DRD-26 (Calculated — descriptive classification is not normative coverage).** Q-β classifies the rationale as a
ground (QBD-05) and expressly adds no requirement (QBD-09; T-3). A classification of retained content cannot establish
that a requirement to retain it exists, and it does not make P-G2's obligation any more grounded in text than GRP-27
found it. **P-1 is not answered by Q-β.** Reading "the baseline retains a ground" as "a ground must be retained" is
exactly the slide T-3 forecloses.

**DRD-27 (Calculated — P-G0's description changed, not its authority or selection status).** QBD-17 records that
P-G0's name is inaccurate over the report-only subset and that its availability is **not** adjudicated there; QBD-23
keeps DQ-2 and all three positions unselected and unranked. So P-3's answer cannot be "P-G0 is false, therefore a
prohibition follows", and P-2 cannot be answered by treating P-G0 as already excluded. Whether P-G0 remains available —
and at what cost, given QAD-18's finding that retaining no cause would be non-compliance with D-3b — is **DQ-2's own
question** (PGR-28; QAD-18).

**DRD-28 (Calculated — the extent remains unestablished, so no coverage figure can be inferred).** QBD-21: the
decision fixes a classification, not an extent; both boundaries are undrawn. Neither P-2's class sizes nor "how much of
the lifecycle stream carries a ground" can be read off Q-β, and no answer to DQ-2 should quote a figure.

**DRD-29 (Observed — nothing else in Q-β bears on DQ-2).** The decision's realised consequences are confined to the
name, the accuracy of "the baseline retains no ground", the constraint said to attach, P-G0's description and how a
DQ-2 answer would be phrased (QBD-19). Of those, only the last touches DQ-2, and it touches the **phrasing** of an
answer, not which answer is right (QBD-18).

Recording "no ground requirement" without T-3 is the ambiguity DRD-12 and DRD-24 exist to prevent.
