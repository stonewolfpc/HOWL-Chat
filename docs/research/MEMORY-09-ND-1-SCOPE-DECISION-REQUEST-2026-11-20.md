## 1. Why the identity comes before the answer

**NSC-01 (Calculated — the problem is not a missing option space, it is two subjects under one name).** The readiness
brief found ND-1 used for two different contents, both dated 2026-11-19: **C-1**, status semantics — fold behaviour, the
`established`/`active` vocabulary, and the status stream's ordering (A2-41…A2-43; DS-09) — and **C-2**, transition
authority — "who may cause a lifecycle change" (CEV-28, CEV-34), while the causation records assign authority to
**DN-6a/DN-6b** (A2-05, A2-40, DS-06, SD-05, SD-23).

**NSC-02 (Calculated — so a decision request without a scope answer would pre-select by drafting).** Whichever content the
options happened to address would become ND-1 by implication. A scope determination prevents that: it fixes the subject
while answering nothing about it.

**NSC-03 (Calculated — a scope determination is itself a boundary decision).** It names what a later question ranges over
and what it does not. It creates no fold rule, key, mapping, event, actor, predicate or schema, and it settles no
substantive question (NDR-27's S-4, applied here).

**NSC-04 (Observed — what is not being asked).** Not whether a fold rule should exist; not how status events are ordered;
not how `established` and `active` map; not who may cause what; not whether a validity basis is required. Each of those
remains open and unauthorised (NDR-19; NDR-30).

## 2. The material the decision ranges over

**NSC-05 (Observed — the four candidate contents, as established).**

| # | Content | The question it would later ask | Its missing artefact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N-a** | **fold semantics** | what a later lifecycle event does to current state | no fold rule for the status stream (A2-41; DS-09) |
| **N-b** | **vocabulary** | how `established` (event) and `active` (status) relate; where `provisional` lives | no stated mapping; no typed representation of formation (A2-43; ODBC-16/17) |
| **N-c** | **ordering** | whether the status stream needs an order key, and which | no order key (DS-09) |
| **N-d** | **authority** | who may cause a lifecycle change | no authority rule (A2-40; DS-06) |

**NSC-06 (Calculated — the only structural relations among them).** **N-c is conditional on N-a** — an order key matters
only if the fold is order-sensitive, and DS-09 explicitly declined to decide whether it is (NDR-15). **N-a and N-b are
separable in principle** — a mapping can be decided without a fold, and a fold could be stated over an informal mapping —
though neither entails the other (NDR-14). **N-d is independent of all three**: no fold supplies an authority condition,
and no authority rule supplies a fold (NDR-16).

**NSC-07 (Observed — N-d already has two other occupants).** DN-6a ("who may cause a character to forget") and DN-6b ("who
may retract content or another character's account") are open and not implied by DN-4 (DS-06; OD-22). Whether "who may
cause a lifecycle change" reaches supersession, correction, formation and scope changes as well is **not stated**
(NDR-18).

**NSC-08 (Observed — `active`/`established` sits inside N-b, and is also a preserved item).** ODL-29 preserves
`active`/`established` as its own open item; A2-43(ii) records the same issue as a vocabulary gap. A scope answer must
therefore say whether ND-1 **absorbs** it, **defers** it, or **preserves it separately** — otherwise one question acquires
two homes (NDR-28).

## 3. The scope options

**NSC-09 (Calculated — five shapes, stated as boundaries, none recommended).**

| # | Scope answer | What ND-1 would later range over | What it would not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S-1** | **semantics only** | N-a, and whichever of N-b/N-c the answer names | any authority question; N-d stays with DN-6a/6b |
| **S-2** | **authority only** | N-d | folds, vocabulary, ordering — which would then need a home |
| **S-3** | **all four, as one question** | N-a…N-d together | nothing by name; but a later decision would have to split them, as DQ-2 did |
| **S-4** | **all four, as separately tracked parts** | four parts, decided on their own terms | nothing; the parts keep separate records and separate authorisations |
| **S-5** | **status quo — ND-1 left undefined** | nothing is fixed | every content remains ambiguous, and the ND-1 → DQ-4 edge stays conditional |

**NSC-10 (Calculated — S-1 has internal variants, and they are not equivalent).** "Semantics only" could mean N-a alone,
N-a with N-b, N-b and N-c without N-a, or all three. Each names a different boundary, and the request does not assume the
widest or the narrowest: a scope answer of S-1 must say which of N-a/N-b/N-c it covers (NSC-16, C-1).

**NSC-11 (Calculated — S-2's consequence is a naming problem, not a substantive one).** If ND-1 is authority only, the
semantics contents do not disappear; they lose their label. This record does not name a replacement label, because naming
one would be creating a tracked question (NSC-03).

**NSC-12 (Calculated — S-3 and S-4 differ only in how the answer is recorded, and that difference is the one that
matters later).** Both cover the same ground. S-3 leaves a single question whose parts must be separated when decided; S-4
separates them now. DQ-2's precedent shows both are workable — its three parts were separated before it was answered
(DRD-02, DQD-08).

**NSC-13 (Calculated — S-5 is a real option, not a straw man).** Leaving ND-1 undefined keeps every content available and
commits nothing. Its cost is that the ambiguity persists and the DQ-4 dependency stays conditional (NDR-24), which is a
consequence, not an argument against it.

**NSC-14 (Observed — none of the five decides anything substantive).** Under every option, no fold rule, mapping, key,
actor, predicate or schema exists that did not exist before. The options differ in **what a later question would be
about**, not in what is required.

prevents the reverse error: keeping DPD-10 in force after a scope answer that removes its premise.
