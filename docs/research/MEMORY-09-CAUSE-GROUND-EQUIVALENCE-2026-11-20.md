**CGE-06 (Calculated — the consequence: no definitional test is available).** "Ground" is not canon vocabulary

be settled by comparing definitions — **neither term has one**. It can only be settled by asking what each does, who
supplies it, how it is typed, and whether one can exist without the other. That is the method of §3.

## 2. What fixes each term

**CGE-07 (Source-derived — cause).** Fixed by D-3b: *"Such events must retain **its actual cause** and independently
identifiable occurrence."* SD-02 reads this as the cause requirement. SD-18 infers that a cause descriptor "names
the *actor or mechanism* that caused the event", and states that its shape is unsettled — "whether it is a field, a
relation or part of an event envelope" is not decided. So the cause's **content** is fixed (actor or mechanism), its
**form** is not, and its **author** is not discussed at all.

**CGE-08 (Observed — ground).** Fixed by nothing canonical. TGP-16 defines it functionally — "a resolvable reference
to a fact stating **why the transition occurred**" — and TGP-20 adds that it is self-reported by the party whose act
it explains. Both are inferences from the discussion, not from a provision.

**CGE-09 (Calculated — the asymmetry that shapes the whole question).** One term has an **owner decision and no
canonical vocabulary**; the other has **neither**. So there is no authoritative definition of either, and any claim
of equivalence or non-equivalence must rest on role, provenance, typing and coverage — not on wording.

**CGE-10 (Source-derived — the one place canon enumerates a reason-bearing record).** v9 §6, "Promotion lifecycle",
`:168`:

> "**Act** — an authorised actor performs an explicit promotion, recorded with actor, authority reference, timestamp
> with offset, source, target domain, declared applicability, basis, optional rationale."

**CGE-11 (Source-derived — and separates evidence from authority in the same section).** v9 `:174`–`:175`:

> "**Bases:** any attributable basis may be cited, including a disclosure. Disclosure is evidence, not authority;
> authority is O-8a."

**CGE-12 (Calculated — the slot structure, which is the strongest evidence available).** v9 §6's record keeps
**four** things apart that this discussion has been tempted to merge:

| v9 slot | Question it answers | Nearest of the three concepts |
|---|---|---|
| `actor` | who performed the act | **cause-like** (partial — a mechanism can cause without an actor) |
| `source` | what record the act drew on | not one of the three; the input, not the basis |
| `basis` + `rationale` | what was cited, and what reason was given | **ground-like** |
| `authority reference` + `declared applicability` | under what authority, over what scope | **validity basis** |

**CGE-13 (Inferred — what the slot structure establishes).** Where the architecture is explicit, it assigns
cause-like, ground-like and authority information to **different slots**, and gives them different obligations: the
actor and authority reference are recorded unconditionally, the basis is recorded unconditionally, and the
**rationale is explicitly optional**. There is **no slot that serves two of the three roles**. That is evidence that
the distinction is not an artifact of this discussion.

**CGE-14 (Calculated — and the ground concept itself splits).** v9 provides **two** ground candidates, not one:
`basis` (an attributable citation — required) and `rationale` (the actor's stated reason — optional). They differ in
attribution and in obligatoriness. So even the word "ground" has no single canonical referent, which is a second
reason R-1 cannot be settled lexically.

## 3. Four tests of conceptual equivalence

**CGE-15 (Calculated — T-1, the question test).** A cause answers *what produced this event*; a ground answers *what
basis was claimed for this change* (GRP-06/09). These are different questions, and a complete answer to one leaves
the other open. **Result: distinct.**

**CGE-16 (Calculated — T-2, the provenance test).** A ground is authored by the party whose act it explains (TGP-20):
it is testimony about basis. A cause, as SD-18 describes it, is a report of the **actor or mechanism** produced by the
recording system. Under I17 the difference is load-bearing: authority must trace to "the conditions under which it was
established" (`:305`), and *who asserts an artifact, and on what access*, determines what it can support.
**Result: distinct — conditional on the cause being system-reported rather than actor-reported (CGE-21/§4).**

**CGE-17 (Calculated — T-3, the typing test).** Where canon types a basis at all, it types it as `record_cited` or
`actor_declaration` (K13), or as "any attributable basis … including a disclosure" (v9 `:174`). A cause's content
type is **an actor or a mechanism** — neither a citation nor a declaration. **Result: distinct**, with the caveat
that K13 and v9 §6 are promotion-scoped (CGE-22).

**CGE-18 (Calculated — T-4, the coverage test).** Neither entails the other:

| Situation | Cause present? | Ground possible? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| statement-less event, no basis stated (e.g. expiry, scheduler loss) | **required** (D-3b) | **no** — nothing was claimed | the cause is a fact about production; no basis was offered |
| statement-bearing event, basis recordable | **not required** (D-3b does not reach it) | **yes** | a basis can be stated without any cause being retained |
| an act with both (a correction caused by a cited record) | present | present | the two coincide **without becoming one thing** |

**Result: not coextensive in either direction.**

**CGE-19 (Calculated — the four-test result).**

| Test | Result | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| T-1 question | distinct | strong — definitional, no scope caveat |
| T-2 provenance | distinct **if** the cause is system-reported | **conditional** — the condition is §4 |
| T-3 typing | distinct | moderate — promotion-scoped |
| T-4 coverage | not coextensive | strong — follows from D-3b's own scope |

**CGE-20 (Inferred — the conceptual answer).** **No: the cause is not a ground under another name.** Three of the four
tests are independent of the promotion material, and the one conditional test is resolved in §4. The conclusion is
about concepts, not about retained content — R-3 is answered separately at §5.

**CGE-21 (Observed — the condition attached to T-2).** T-2 assumes the cause is a report *by the recording system*
about an origin it can observe. SD-18 does not say that. If a cause can only be obtained from the party whose act it
explains, the artifact's provenance is the party, not the system, and T-2 weakens to a distinction of **role** without
a distinction of **source**. That case is §4.

**CGE-22 (Observed — the promotion precedent's scope, again).** v9 §6 and K13/K15/K17 govern **promotion**. They are
cited here for the **conceptual partition** they display — separate slots for actor, basis, rationale and authority —
not as obligations for lifecycle transitions. Nothing in this section transfers them (GRP-46/47), and the owner's
boundary (A-5) is respected: the analogy informs, it does not authorise.

**CGE-23 (Calculated — what T-3's caveat does and does not weaken).** T-3 relies on the only place the architecture
types a basis. If that material were excluded entirely, T-1 and T-4 would still yield "not equivalent as concepts".
T-3 is corroborative, not load-bearing.

## 4. The one case where the distinction narrows: report-only causes

**CGE-24 (Inferred — two kinds of cause, distinguished by access).** A cause can be known in two ways, and D-3b does
not distinguish them:

| Access | Example | What the recorded cause is |
|---|---|---|
| **independently observable** | a clock expiry; a scheduler dropping a record; a review process whose finding exists in its own record | a **fact about the process**, reportable without the actor's cooperation |
| **report-only** | an operator's stated intention; a narrator grant's purpose; a model's own decision rationale | the **actor's account**, available only because the actor supplied it |

**CGE-25 (Inferred — in the report-only case the artifact's content is testimony).** If the only access to the cause
is the actor's account, then the recorded cause is the actor saying why the act happened — which is the same
**content** a ground would carry. It remains a report of origin in form, but its substance is a claim about basis.

**CGE-26 (Inferred — in the observable case it is not).** A clock expiry's cause is not a claim about why the change
was warranted; it is a fact about elapsed time. Recording it does not put a justification into the stream, and no
reader can mistake it for one.

**CGE-27 (Unestablished — which case the corpus intends).** D-3b says "its **actual** cause" and says nothing about
how the cause is known. SD-04 forbids fabricated evidence and inferred statement positions, but it says **nothing
about inferred causes**. So it is not established whether an unobservable cause may be recorded at all, whether it
must be marked as reported rather than observed, or whether a report-only cause is admissible as "actual".

**CGE-28 (Calculated — the hinge's real answer).** **Not equivalent as concepts; convergent in content only for the
report-only subset.** The equivalence is therefore **partial and conditional**, and the condition is a property of
the individual event (how its cause is known), not a property of any position or decision.

**CGE-29 (Unestablished — and the subset's boundary is undrawn).** "Report-only" is not the same property as
"statement-less" (TGP-11): a statement-less event may have an independently observable cause, and a statement-bearing
event's cause may be report-only. So the two boundaries are distinct, and **both are undrawn**. Their overlap is
unestablished.

**CGE-30 (Calculated — the two extremes, both consistent with the text).** If the report-only subset is **empty**,
P-G1 never retains ground-like content and the hinge resolves cleanly in favour of non-equivalence. If the subset is
**the whole statement-less class**, P-G1 always retains ground-like content for that class. Nothing in the corpus
chooses between these, and this record does not.

**CGE-31 (Inferred — why this is a finding rather than an evasion).** The reason the answer is conditional is
structural: D-3b fixes **what** a cause is (actor or mechanism) and not **how it is known**, and I17 makes the second
question decisive. The gap is in the obligation's specification, not in the analysis.

## 5. What this does to P-G0 and P-G1

**CGE-32 (Calculated — they always differ in one artifact).** P-G1 retains a cause where the event is statement-less;
P-G0 retains none. That difference is real **regardless of classification** (GRP-24's M-1). So P-G0 and P-G1 are not
the same policy under any reading.

**CGE-33 (Calculated — they differ on the ground axis only over the report-only subset).** Away from that subset, the
cause is a fact about production and P-G1 retains no ground. Over it, P-G1 retains something whose content is
ground-like. So the ground-axis difference between P-G0 and P-G1 is **exactly the size of the report-only subset**,
which is unestablished (CGE-29/30).

**CGE-34 (Inferred — the choice is partly a classification choice, not a quantity choice).** To the extent that
subset is non-empty, choosing between P-G0 and P-G1 involves deciding **what the cause counts as** — a system report
or an actor claim. That is not "how much to retain"; it is "how to label one artifact over one class of events". The
apparent distance between the positions is therefore **partly an artifact of an unsettled classification**, and
settling it may shrink that distance without making the positions identical.

**CGE-35 (Calculated — P-G0's availability is conditional on the classification).** GRP-21 stands: if the cause is a
ground, P-G0 must displace D-3b's reach to remain accurate; if it is not, P-G0 is coherent and differs from P-G1 only
in M-1 content. **A-2 holds either way** — nothing in P-G0 permits violating D-3b, and this record does not read it
that way.

**CGE-36 (Inferred — P-G1's ground status is inherited, not chosen).** D-3b decides the cause; the classification
decides what that cause counts as. P-G1 therefore is **not an intermediate policy** (A-1) but a consequence of two
other things: an existing owner decision, and an unresolved classification. That is the precise sense in which the
original three-position framing was misaligned.

**CGE-37 (Calculated — neither affects the validity gap).** Resolving the hinge leaves GRP-31 untouched: axis H is
identical across all three positions, and a cause-classified-as-ground supplies no authority (`:305`; A-3). Nothing
here narrows ND-1.

**CGE-38 (Inferred — nor does classification create authority).** A report-only cause is self-reported even when it is
classified as a ground, so I17's prohibition bites with undiminished force (B-2/B-3; TGP-19). Classification changes
the **label** and the **constraint that attaches**; it does not change the artifact's standing.

**CGE-39 (Observed — an interpretive gap in the text, recorded not repaired).** D-3b contains no "never read as
warrant" clause for the cause — unlike CLV-28's explicit constraint on a ground. So **if** a cause is a ground, the
constraint attaches; if it is not, the cause has no stated interpretive constraint at all. That gap is in the
obligation's text. It is **not** a gap in the owner's position: B-2 already forbids reading a recorded basis as
authority, and this record adds no clause.

**CGE-40 (Calculated — the honest statement of R-3).** P-G0 and P-G1 **always** differ by one artifact (M-1) and
**conditionally** differ on the ground axis, by an amount equal to an undrawn subset. They are therefore neither
equivalent nor separated by a determinate quantity. The owner's "subtle point" is confirmed: **the distinction
affects retained content, and its size is currently unmeasurable.**

**CGE-42 (Observed — what it would not settle).** ND-1; the validity predicate; the admissible-transition set;
whether any position is preferable; whether a ground must be retained stream-wide; DQ-2; closure; determinism;
`active`/`established`; §8.1; P-EVAL; C′; OD-g; D-3b's reach into lifecycle transitions. **None of these is narrowed
by this record, and the hinge can be resolved without touching any of them** — which is why isolating it is the
right next step rather than selecting a position.

**CGE-43 (Calculated — the question the resolution would itself be).** The classification is not derivable from text:
one would have to decide either (a) that a cause is a **system report of origin** and never a ground — which resolves
the hinge in favour of non-equivalence and leaves the report-only case unexplained, or (b) that a report-only cause
**is** a ground — which resolves it in favour of conditional equivalence and makes D-3b a partial ground obligation,
or (c) that the classification stays open, accepting that the two readings coexist and that retained content cannot
be characterised in advance. **None is recommended, ranked or selected here.**

**CGE-44 (Observed — and (c) has a cost that must be stated with it).** Leaving it open means a reader of a lifecycle
record cannot tell whether a recorded cause is a ground, so the CLV-28 constraint's attachment is undecidable and
P-G0/P-G1 cannot be fully characterised. That is a consequence, not an argument against (c).

**CGE-45 (Observed — no requirement is implied).** The finding concerns the **classification of an existing
obligation**, not the addition of one. Nothing here requires a cause to be retained, forbids a ground, mandates a
marker, or creates a row. GRP-27's finding stands: P-G2's stream-wide obligation has no textual basis.

**CGE-46 (Observed — nothing is promoted from `[P]`).** K-R83/K-R84 stay proposed (B-6); §11.1 and §11.3 stay `[P]`.
v9 §6 is canonical text with no `[P]` marker, cited as architecture, not as one of the 118 requirements.

**CGE-47 (Observed — the choice remains unmade).** P-G0, P-G1 and P-G2 remain unranked, unselected and uncharacterised
