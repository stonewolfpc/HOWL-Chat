**DSC-03 (Calculated — a clarification is not a decision in disguise).** Characterising a question's answer space does

**DSC-04 (Observed — what stays outside).** ND-1, the predicate/set relationship, QAD-19 and the report-only boundary

**DSC-05 (Calculated — the three are different kinds of question, which is the record's first result).**

| | **DQ-3** | **DQ-5** | **Prohibition** |
|---|---|---|---|

kind, only reach.

## 2. DQ-3 — "recorded as stated and never evaluated"

**DSC-06 (Source-derived — the question).** GRP-53's DQ-3: "If retained, is it required to be recorded **as stated and
never evaluated**?" Its stated dependency is "CLV-28; the K15 precedent only as analogy (GRP-46)", and what it cannot
borrow from is "the promotion path — its scope is promotion, and its authority clause presumes ND-1".

**DSC-07 (Observed — the question's condition is now satisfied).** DQ-3 was written as conditional — "**if**
retained". Retention exists: D-3b requires the cause, and where the cause is report-only it is also a ground (QBD-14).
So the question has a subject today, and its condition does not need a prior decision (DPD-15).

**DSC-08 (Calculated — DQ-3 is two constraints, and they are separable).** The phrase joins two different things:

| Component | What it would require | Distinct because |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) "recorded as stated"** | the actor's stated basis is preserved as stated, attributed to its author | a fidelity/attribution constraint — it concerns **form** |
| **(b) "never evaluated"** | the record's sufficiency is not assessed — no judgement that the basis was adequate | a constraint on **use** — it concerns what a reader or the fold may conclude |

Nothing in the corpus requires the two to travel together. A decision could adopt (a) without (b), or (b) without (a);
this record states the separability and adopts neither.

**DSC-09 (Calculated — what a "required" answer would settle).** That the retained ground carries (a), (b) or both as a
constraint; and, if so, that a reader or the fold treating it otherwise would be in breach. It would also fix whether the
constraint is stated as a requirement at all — which is why its answer can change the requirement set (DSC-05).

**DSC-10 (Observed — what a "not required" answer would settle).** That no such constraint exists beyond what already
applies: ODC-07's owner-level boundary — the report establishes no truth, authority or validity (ODC-07; QBD-15) —
remains, and CLV-28 stays an **inference** rather than a constraint (QBR-22; QBD-16). A "not required" answer does not
say the content may be treated as a warrant; ODC-07 already forecloses that, and I17 governs regardless (`:305`).

**DSC-11 (Calculated — what DQ-3 cannot settle).** Not validity (axis H, GRP-31); not ND-1; not whether grounds are
retained (DQ-2 settled: no requirement beyond D-3b); not whether the constraint is enforced or marked (ODC-29: the limit
is not a check); not whether D-3b's text gains a clause — that would be a textual amendment, and ODC-21 records the gap
as remaining **in the text**. It also cannot borrow the K15 precedent: same subject, different scope, and the transfer
verdict stands (GRP-46/GRP-47).

**DSC-12 (Unestablished — and whether DQ-3 is statable without a carrier term).** "Recorded as stated" presupposes a
record that can hold a statement verbatim. Whether that is specifiable without deciding the rationale's separability
within the artifact is not established (ODC-15), and no carrier is authorised (ODC-09). The question is therefore
answerable as a **constraint**, but its precise subject may not be — recorded as a limit, not a reason to answer it.

## 3. DQ-5 — "prospectively only"

**DSC-13 (Source-derived — the question).** GRP-53's DQ-5: "Does retention apply **prospectively only**, and is that
stated?" Its stated dependency is "GRP-36's time asymmetry", and what it cannot borrow from is "nothing — this is a scope
term of the decision itself".

**DSC-14 (Observed — its subject is now thin, and that is a finding).** DQ-2 added no requirement, so the only retention
obligation in force is D-3b, an existing obligation applying to events as they occur. DQ-5's present subject is therefore
**narrowed** to D-3b, and whether anything remains to decide about it is itself unestablished (DPD-17).

**DSC-15 (Calculated — the question has two readings, and only one is about time).**

| Reading | What it would fix | Is it about time? |
|---|---|---|
| **(i) adoption scope** | which events are within the obligation — those from adoption forward, or also earlier ones | **yes** |
| **(ii) post-hoc completion** | whether a basis missing at write time may be supplied later, and how it would be marked | **no** — it is about completion, not reach |

The corpus's evidence bears on both but states neither: "a basis written after the fact is not a record of a
contemporaneous claim" (GRP-36), and the omission "is paid at write time" (PGM-26). That is a **fact about when the cost
falls**, expressly not an argument for retaining anything (GRP-36; ODL-17).

**DSC-16 (Calculated — what a decision on DQ-5 could settle).** Reading (i): the temporal reach of whichever obligation
is in force. Reading (ii): whether completion is addressed at all, and if so, whether a later-supplied basis is
distinguishable from a contemporaneous one. A decision may answer (i) and leave (ii) open; nothing forces both.

**DSC-17 (Calculated — what DQ-5 cannot settle).** Not whether retention exists (DQ-2); not whether completion would be
**fabrication** — M-3 forbids fabricated evidence and inferred statement positions (SD-04), and whether a genuine later
recollection recorded as later falls inside or outside that prohibition is **Unestablished**, not answered by a scope
term (DSC-18). Not ND-1, not validity, not any extent.

**DSC-18 (Unestablished — the seam between scope and prohibition).** "May be supplied later" and "must not be
manufactured" are adjacent: a scope term that permitted completion would not thereby make a manufactured basis
acceptable, and M-3 would still bind. The record notes the seam and does not resolve it, because resolving it would
require stating what distinguishes a later recollection from a fabricated one — which is a different decision.

**DSC-19 (Observed — a "prospective only" answer is not retroactive authorisation).** If DQ-5 were answered that
retention applies from adoption forward, nothing would be authorised for earlier events: no basis would be created, and
the asymmetry would remain a fact about the record rather than a rule about what may now be added (GRP-36).

## 4. The prohibition option

**DSC-20 (Source-derived — the question).** Whether retaining a basis **beyond D-3b** should be **forbidden** — a new
negative constraint, not a reading of P-G0. P-G0's own statement contains no modal verb of obligation or prohibition, and
its writer obligation is recorded as "none" (PGM-08, PGM-10, PGM-29). A prohibition "cannot be obtained by re-reading
P-G0" and would need "its own record and its own authority" (PGM-30).

**DSC-21 (Calculated — what a prohibition would have to specify, and none of it is given).**

| Term | The question it fixes | State |
|---|---|---|
| **subject** | whom the prohibition binds — writers, the fold, the schema, or readers | not specified (PGM-20) |
| **prohibited conduct** | what exactly is forbidden — the transition carrying a basis beyond D-3b, or any retention of one | not specified (PGM-20) |
| **consequence** | what follows on breach — refusal at write, an error, nothing enforced | not specified; **ODL-39 is preserved and not opened** |

**DSC-22 (Calculated — what a prohibition could settle).** Whether the baseline is permissive **by decision** rather than
by omission — the one question P-G0's silence leaves open (PGM-29). It would convert "nothing forbids recording" from an
absence into a rule, and thereby make the arrangement's permissiveness explicit.

**DSC-23 (Calculated — what a prohibition cannot settle).** It creates **no permission** and no mandate: recording is
already unprohibited (DQD-13). It supplies no validity basis and does not touch ND-1 (GRP-31). It does not make P-G0
restrictive by interpretation, because it **is** that act, not a reading of it (PGM-30). And it does not decide whether a
basis may be *preserved in the conversation record* — the character's utterance was never within the retention axis's
scope (PGM-23/PGM-24); only the transition's **link** to it would be at issue.

**DSC-24 (Unestablished — whether the conduct can be stated without a carrier term).** Post-Q-β the ground is **not** a
separate carrier: it is a reading of the artifact D-3b already requires (QBD-06; ODC-28). A write-time prohibition
therefore cannot be phrased as "no field for a basis"; its conduct would have to be specified in terms of content, and
whether that is possible without presupposing a carrier is not established (ODC-15; ODC-09). Recorded as a limit of the
option, not as an argument against it.

## 5. Interactions among the three, and what none can settle

**DSC-25 (Calculated — DQ-3 and ODC-07 are adjacent, not identical).** ODC-07 constrains what the report
**establishes** (nothing); DQ-3 constrains how it is **handled** (as stated, not evaluated). A reader may therefore think
DQ-3 is already answered. It is not: a boundary on what content can support is not a rule about whether its sufficiency
may be assessed, and CLV-28's constraint remains an inference (QBD-16). Stated so the two are not collapsed.

**DSC-26 (Calculated — DQ-3 and a prohibition interact, without either deciding the other).** If a prohibition were
adopted forbidding a basis beyond D-3b, DQ-3's surface would shrink to the ground D-3b's own artifact carries — the
constraint would still have a subject, but a smaller one. If no prohibition exists (as now), DQ-3's subject is the
retained ground wherever it occurs. Neither answer follows from the other, and this record does not order them.

**DSC-27 (Calculated — DQ-5 and a prohibition interact on time).** A prohibition adopted now would have to state whether
it reaches existing records or only future writes — DQ-5's reading (i) applied to a prohibition rather than a retention
obligation. That is a term of the prohibition, not an answer to DQ-5.

**DSC-28 (Calculated — the shared boundary: what none of the three can settle).**

| **ND-1** — who may cause a lifecycle change | none of the three concerns permission to cause (QAD-12; DQD-10) |

| **QAD-19** — which transitions are statement-less | a different boundary, undrawn and not authorised as work |

| **axis H** | identical across every position; not an owner question (GRP-31; DPD-25) |

**DSC-29 (Observed — no cross-question ranking).** DSC-26 and DSC-27 state interactions, not priority. Each of the three
